# World at War map ports

How a compiled World at War (T4) custom map becomes a Kino custom map (2026-10-04, first port: Cheese Cube Unlimited, `nazi_zombie_ccube_u`).

**Pipeline**
1. The map ships as an NSIS installer holding `<map>.ff`, `<map>.iwd` and a mod folder. Unpack it with `7z x` (never run it), put the files outside the repo, e.g. `~/projects/waw-cheese-cube/`.
2. OpenAssetTools (`~/.local/opt/OpenAssetTools`) loads T4 zones and dumps models (GLB), images (DDS), materials, map entities (`.ents`), scripts and weapons. It had **no dumper for the world mesh** (`gfxworld`), so `.tools/oat/GfxWorldDumperT4.{h,cpp}` adds one: copy both into `src/ObjWriting/Game/T4/GfxWorld/`, add `#include "GfxWorld/GfxWorldDumperT4.h"` and `RegisterAssetDumper(std::make_unique<gfx_world::DumperT4>());` to `ObjWriterT4.cpp`, run `./generate.sh`, then `make -C build -j6 config=release_x64 UnlinkerCli`. It writes `<zone>.d3dbsp.obj` (one object per surface, only the vertices a surface uses), `.smodels.json` (placed static models) and `.brushmodels.json` (door/barrier brush models).
3. Dump with the game's archives on the search path so stock textures resolve: `Unlinker --search-path "<WaW>/main;<map folder>" --model-format GLB --image-format DDS --include-assets xmodel,mapents,image,material,rawfile,gfxworld -o <out>/?zone? <map>.ff`.
4. `python3 .tools/build_waw_map.py <dump dir> export/web/mods/maps/<id>` writes `<id>.glb` (CoD inches, Y up, scale 1) with the markers. Per-map settings live in `.tools/waw_maps/<zone>.json`.
5. Add an entry to `mods/maps.json` (`dir`, `model`, `scale: 1`). The map folder gets a `.gitignore` for everything but itself: **ripped game data stays out of git** (the maps.json entry is tracked, the files are not).

**What the build script does**
- World surfaces become one mesh grouped by material. Each triangle is wound to agree with its vertex normals (the dumped winding is not uniform, and the navmesh builder decides walkable floors from it). Textures: DDS to WebP (max 512) through ffmpeg, embedded with `EXT_texture_webp`. Material name to colour map comes from the material JSON; alpha test or glass sets the glTF alpha mode.
- **Brush models** (every model except 0: doors, moving stairs, plates, the rising plane) store their geometry relative to their entity's `origin` (and `angles`); the build script places each one. Without that, all 268 pile up at (0,0,0) (a black wall on the ground floor, doors in the middle). The dumper's V coordinate is OBJ-style (flipped), so the build script flips it back for glTF (posters were upside down).
- Oversized surfaces (a plane tens of thousands of units wide) are clipped to the map footprint: the game sizes its navmesh cells from the bounds, and one huge plane made them too coarse for corridors (the zombies had no navmesh on most floors).
- Doors: each `zombie_door`/`zombie_debris` trigger's `target` names `script_brushmodel`s; their surfaces (via the brush model indices) become a `DOOR_<zone>_<cost>` mesh. Debris doors open `enter_zoneN`; plain doors take the nearest zone by height.
- Markers: `initial_spawn_points` to `PLAYER_SPAWN`, `<zone>_spawners_rise` to `ZSPAWN_<zone>` (`start_zone` is `start`), `weapon_upgrade` to `WALLBUY_` through `WEAPON_MAP` (the map's own weapons are not in the game), `zombie_vending` to `PERK_` (only the four supported perks), `zombie_vending_upgrade` to `PAP`, the start chest to `BOX_start`. Marker yaw is the CoD yaw about the up axis.
- Static props (placed `xmodel`s) are baked in with their placement; skinned models (characters), `viewmodel_` and `weapon_` models are skipped.
- Config keys: `dropSpawnersBelow` (spawn points on a floor the navmesh does not connect to would leave zombies stuck, which blocks rounds), `extraSpawns` (replacements, picked from floor points that can path to the player), `rising` (material, delay, speed: the surface becomes a `RISING_<delay>_<speed>` mesh).

**Cheese Cube Unlimited**
- The gimmick (from `nazi_zombie_ccube_u.gsc`): `cheese_bottom` rises 4600 units over 2000 s (2.3 units/s) after 97 s plus a 3 s alarm; touching it kills even with Quick Revive. Ported as `RISING_100_2.3` (see `mods/README.md`; engine side is `updateRising` in `custom-world.js` and the hazard block in `maps/custom.js`).
- Start-zone ground spawners (z below 30) were on a floor island the navmesh does not connect to the stairs: dropped, and four reachable spiral-stair points added.
- Verified in a real browser: no map warnings, zombies spawn, chase and attack, the cheese is at height 70 at t=130 s, standing below it ends the game.
- Player feel found in playtests: the stairs have 12-unit risers and the capsule is flagged off the floor for several frames of each step-up, so the movement mod's stair offset missed most of the rise and the camera lurched. Fixed in `mods/movement/mod.js` behind the map entry `stairSmoothing` (about 60% less camera jerk climbing at `stairSmoothRate` 5, measured by walking a computed path). Door collision is grown with `doorMargin`/`doorHeadroom` (the straight-line leak test could not get past any door even without it, so this is a precaution, not a proven fix).
- Rounds: every script in the map zone, the mod zone (`mod.ff`) and the patch zone uses the standard `round_wait` (a round ends when all zombies are dead). The timer-driven rounds the map is said to use are not in any dumped script, so they are not ported yet.
- **Moving platforms**: `step_move1` and `step_move15` to `24` are the only ones that exist in the map (the script's other `step_move`s reference missing entities). They are `MOVER_` meshes with `movers.json` timings from the script (`cfg.movers`; CoD moveX/Y/Z become game x, -z, y). They slide out of and back into the cube wall, so riding one into the wall is the intended hazard. Platforms are not in the navmesh, so zones 2 and up are not connected to the start for zombies even with every door open (checked), hence `nearbySpawns`. The plates (`plate1`-`8`) belong to the older zone script and are left static; `step_rotate1` does not exist.
- **Easter eggs ported** (`mods/cheese-cube`, only on this map; pieces are `ENT_<targetname>` meshes and `entities.json`, built from `cfg.entityMeshes`/`entityProps`): the power switch (carry the panel, handle and wire to the switch, then it works; the original gates the perks on it), the five Cheese Brothers (shoot with the cheese gun, here the Ray Gun: 2000 each, 4000 for all five) the trivia (the screen's seven questions: shoot the answer plate, B B C D B D A, a wrong answer locks it for two minutes; the reward, the War Machine suit, is replaced by an RPG), the dial puzzle (three dials start at 5 and step down one per press: right 8, middle 1, left 2; solving it slides `thor_ending_door` up 300, a `manual` mover) and the ending (25000 at the top, locked until the puzzle is solved: a short announcement, then the game is won). Their progress shows in the Tab scoreboard.
- **Iron Man suits** (`mods/cheese-cube/suits.js`, cut down from `_iron_man_mk*.gsc`): six parts lie around the map (`pick_<part>_trig`; helmet, body, two arms, two legs); carry each to the Hall of Armors (`place_<part>_trig`) and the six holos turn blue. Then the stands (`give_iron` Mk3 7500, `give_mk1` 5000, `give_mk4` 10000, `give_mk5` 12500, `give_mk6` 15000, `give_wm` War Machine 10000 and it also needs the trivia reward) sell the suits; use a stand again to wear it (one at a time: health Mk1 350 / Mk3, Mk4, Mk6 285 / Mk5 200 / WM 320 through a wrapped `session.maxHealth`, walking speed scaled, Mk1 cannot sprint, the suit's weapon on the first wear: Ray Gun or the RPG-class `m72_law_zm`) and use it again to take it off; it recharges for 20 s on its stand, and dying removes it. The props are the `entityProps` `pick_*`, `place_*`, `rotate_*`, `mk1/4/5/6`, `wm_model`. Also: hold Space in the air while suited to fly (thrust 1500 up to 260 u/s, power 22/s down, 16/s back on the ground, bar under the screen); a suit sinks out of its stand on wear and rises on return; the Hall of Armors holos spin about their own centre; owning all six hides `malibu_wall` and `purchase_armors`, then `give_mk16` slides `mk16_door` away and the `unlock_mk16` scanner kills you after 4 s (the map's joke: only the author's friends are authorized, so the Mk16 suit itself is not ported). Co-op: quest parts (power switch and Iron Man), builds and suit purchases are shared through `kino.coop` `cc` messages (late joiners miss earlier events). The Tab scoreboard draws buildables as part slots (`addCollectible({parts})`), without perks or equipment. Not ported: the Mk16 suit, the suit HUD art, the stand brushes.
- **Smaller eggs and sounds** (`mods/cheese-cube/eggs.js`, from the map's runtime script in its iwd): Cap's shield (four plates, `cap_shield<n>_trig`; shoot the one under the shield and it flies to the next over 2 s; after the fourth one of three Avengers themes plays, cycling), Stan Lee (`stan_lee_egg`: shoot him and he kills the shooter, then he and Clementine complain), the poster target, the cheese alarm at 97 s, the PhD and Double Dip jingles every 90/80 s after the power (volume by distance), JARVIS voices (hall of armors, suit up, charging, a second suit, all six bought, power low) and the ending (thor_end thunder, the hammer rises 100 then 70 units, black screen, the closing cards, game over after about 46 s). The sounds are the map's own wavs from `nazi_zombie_ccube_u.iwd` converted to ogg into the map folder's `sounds/` (git-ignored). The credits ("Special Thanks to" and five names, one card at a time) run 4 s after any game over, as in the original. Not ported: `thompson_troll` (only for one player name), `cheats`/`anti_cheat`.
- **Extra perks** (`mods/cheese-cube/perks.js`): PHD Flopper (1000, the map's own model `ENT_vending_phd_flopper`, a dive landing explodes for 3500 within 220 units) and Double-Dew (4000, no effect, as in the original; its machine is geometry behind door `zone2_750#5`), both need the power. They add ids to `session.perks` and entries to the engine's perk table through `world.perkInfo`, so the HUD shows them. The original PHD also gave immunity to explosions and falls (not ported).
- **War Machine stand**: its cage (`ENT_wm_cage`, six brushes) sinks 100 at the start and drops away 250 once the trivia is done; `wm_holo_red` turns into `wm_holo` on purchase and both spin; `ENT_stark_tower` is the tower prop in the Hall of Armors.
- Aiming at the trivia plates: they are thin slits stacked 1.7 units apart on a 12-unit screen (the original's trigger brushes), and a shot lands about 0.9 units above the crosshair at ~35 units, so the game gives each plate a contiguous hit band (halfway to its neighbours) and a shot hits only the nearest target. The test aims 0.9 low.
- Not ported: Cap's shields and the other gags, the Mk16 suit and other scripted extras, the `cheese_ending` trigger, the map's custom weapons, the PHD Flopper and Vulture-style perks (no marker), and the lights (the dump has no light data, so the map is lit by the engine defaults).
