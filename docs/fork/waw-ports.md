# World at War map ports

How a compiled World at War (T4) custom map becomes a Kino custom map (2026-10-04, first port: Cheese Cube Unlimited, `nazi_zombie_ccube_u`).

**Pipeline**
1. The map ships as an NSIS installer holding `<map>.ff`, `<map>.iwd` and a mod folder. Unpack it with `7z x` (never run it), put the files outside the repo, e.g. `~/projects/waw-cheese-cube/`.
2. OpenAssetTools (`~/.local/opt/OpenAssetTools`) loads T4 zones and dumps models (GLB), images (DDS), materials, map entities (`.ents`), scripts and weapons. It had **no dumper for the world mesh** (`gfxworld`), so `.tools/oat/GfxWorldDumperT4.{h,cpp}` adds one: copy both into `src/ObjWriting/Game/T4/GfxWorld/`, add `#include "GfxWorld/GfxWorldDumperT4.h"` and `RegisterAssetDumper(std::make_unique<gfx_world::DumperT4>());` to `ObjWriterT4.cpp`, run `./generate.sh`, then `make -C build -j6 config=release_x64 UnlinkerCli`. `.tools/oat/ClipMapDumperT4.{h,cpp}` (2026-10-19) dumps the collision (`<zone>.d3dbsp.clipmap.json`: brushes with contents and their brush model, terrain/patch triangles with materials, static models and their xmodels' collision surfaces): copy both into `src/ObjWriting/Game/T4/ClipMap/` and register `clip_map::DumperT4` and `clip_map::PvsDumperT4` next to the GfxWorld one; dump with `clipmap` in `--include-assets`. The GfxWorld dumper also writes vertex colours (`vc r g b a`). It writes `<zone>.d3dbsp.obj` (one object per surface, only the vertices a surface uses), `.smodels.json` (placed static models) and `.brushmodels.json` (door/barrier brush models).
3. Dump with the game's archives on the search path so stock textures resolve: `Unlinker --search-path "<WaW>/main;<map folder>" --model-format GLB --image-format DDS --include-assets xmodel,mapents,image,material,rawfile,gfxworld -o <out>/?zone? <map>.ff`.
4. `python3 .tools/build_waw_map.py <dump dir> export/web/mods/maps/<id>` writes `<id>.glb` (CoD inches, Y up, scale 1) with the markers. Per-map settings live in `.tools/waw_maps/<zone>.json`.
5. Add an entry to `mods/maps.json` (`dir`, `model`, `scale: 1`). The map folder gets a `.gitignore` for everything but itself: **ripped game data stays out of git** (the maps.json entry is tracked, the files are not).

**What the build script does**
- World surfaces become one mesh grouped by material. Each triangle is wound to agree with its vertex normals (the dumped winding is not uniform, and the navmesh builder decides walkable floors from it). Textures: DDS to WebP (max 512) through ffmpeg, embedded with `EXT_texture_webp`. Material name to colour map comes from the material JSON; alpha test or glass sets the glTF alpha mode.
- **Brush models** (every model except 0: doors, moving stairs, plates, the rising plane) store their geometry relative to their entity's `origin` (and `angles`); the build script places each one. Without that, all 268 pile up at (0,0,0) (a black wall on the ground floor, doors in the middle). The dumper's V coordinate is OBJ-style (flipped), so the build script flips it back for glTF (posters were upside down).
- Oversized surfaces (a plane tens of thousands of units wide) are clipped to the map footprint: the game sizes its navmesh cells from the bounds, and one huge plane made them too coarse for corridors (the zombies had no navmesh on most floors).
- Doors: each `zombie_door`/`zombie_debris` trigger's `target` names `script_brushmodel`s; their surfaces (via the brush model indices) become a `DOOR_<zone>_<cost>` mesh. Debris doors open `enter_zoneN`; plain doors take the nearest zone by height.
- Markers: `initial_spawn_points` to `PLAYER_SPAWN`, `<zone>_spawners_rise` to `ZSPAWN_<zone>` (`start_zone` is `start`), `weapon_upgrade` to `WALLBUY_` through `WEAPON_MAP` (the map's own weapons are not in the game), `zombie_vending` to `PERK_` (only the four supported perks), `zombie_vending_upgrade` to `PAP`, the start chest to `BOX_start`. Marker yaw is the CoD yaw about the up axis.
- Static props (placed `xmodel`s) are baked in with their placement; skinned models (characters), `viewmodel_` and `weapon_` models are skipped.
- Config keys: `dropSpawnersBelow` (spawn points on a floor the navmesh does not connect to would leave zombies stuck, which blocks rounds), `extraSpawns` (replacements, picked from floor points that can path to the player), `rising` (material, delay, speed: the surface becomes a `RISING_<delay>_<speed>` mesh).

**Cheese Cube Unlimited**
- The gimmick (from `nazi_zombie_ccube_u.gsc`): `cheese_bottom` rises 4600 units over 2000 s (2.3 units/s) after 97 s plus a 3 s alarm; touching it kills even with Quick Revive. Ported as `RISING_100_2.3` (see `mods/README.md`; engine side is `updateRising` in `custom-world.js` and the hazard block in `maps/custom.js`).
- Start-zone ground spawners (z below 30) were on a floor island the navmesh does not connect to the stairs: dropped, and four reachable spiral-stair points added.
- Verified in a real browser: no map warnings, zombies spawn, chase and attack, the cheese is at height 70 at t=130 s, standing below it ends the game.
- Player feel found in playtests: the stairs have 12-unit risers and the capsule is flagged off the floor for several frames of each step-up, so the movement mod's stair offset missed most of the rise and the camera lurched. Fixed in `mods/movement/mod.js` behind the map entry `stairSmoothing` (about 60% less camera jerk climbing at `stairSmoothRate` 5, measured by walking a computed path). Door collision is grown with `doorMargin`/`doorHeadroom` (the straight-line leak test could not get past any door even without it, so this is a precaution, not a proven fix).
- Rounds: every script in the map zone, the mod zone (`mod.ff`) and the patch zone uses the standard `round_wait` (a round ends when all zombies are dead). The timer-driven rounds the map is said to use are not in any dumped script, so they are not ported yet.
- **Moving platforms**: `step_move1` and `step_move15` to `24` are the only ones that exist in the map (the script's other `step_move`s reference missing entities). They are `MOVER_` meshes with `movers.json` timings from the script (`cfg.movers`; CoD moveX/Y/Z become game x, -z, y). They slide out of and back into the cube wall, so riding one into the wall is the intended hazard. Platforms are not in the navmesh, so zones 2 and up are not connected to the start for zombies even with every door open (checked), hence `nearbySpawns`. The plates (`plate1`-`8`) belong to the older zone script and are left static; `step_rotate1` does not exist.
- **Easter eggs ported** (`mods/cheese-cube`, only on this map; pieces are `ENT_<targetname>` meshes and `entities.json`, built from `cfg.entityMeshes`/`entityProps`): the power switch (carry the panel, handle and wire to the switch, then it works; the original gates the perks on it), the five Cheese Brothers (shoot with the Cheese Gun: 2000 each, 4000 for all five) the trivia (the screen's seven questions: shoot the answer plate, B B C D B D A, a wrong answer locks it for two minutes; the reward, the War Machine suit, is replaced by an RPG), the dial puzzle (three dials start at 5 and step down one per press: right 8, middle 1, left 2; solving it slides `thor_ending_door` up 300, a `manual` mover) and the ending (25000 at the top, locked until the puzzle is solved: a short announcement, then the game is won). Their progress shows in the Tab scoreboard.

- Not ported: Cap's shields and the other gags, the Mk16 suit and other scripted extras, the `cheese_ending` trigger, the other custom weapons (the Cheese Gun is ported, below), the PHD Flopper and Vulture-style perks (no marker), and the lights (the dump has no light data, so the map is lit by the engine defaults).

## Black Ops III maps (2026-10-07): The Giant

No tool reads BO3's fastfiles here, and Husky 0.8 (map geometry from the running game) only knows the 2022 build, so `.tools/bo3_dump_map.py` reads the loaded map straight from the game's memory (`/proc/<pid>/mem`; BO3 under Proton, ptrace_scope 0). It ports Husky's `BlackOps3.cs` (GPL; struct layouts in the script) with the asset-pool table found for the current build: Husky's `base+0x94093F0` moved to **`base+0x93883F0`** (`--scan` finds it again: the table whose entry +0x80 is the model pool whose first model is `void`, and +0x200 a GfxMap). Pool entries are 0x20 apart: GfxMap is +0x200 (type 16), MapEnts +0x1E0 (type 15: `name`, `entityString` @+8, length @+0x10). The process is the one whose cmdline is `S:\common\Call of Duty Black Ops III\BlackOps3.exe` (comm `Main`); the exe maps at 0x140000000.
- Output in `export_game/bo3/maps/<map>/` (git-ignored): raw positions/vertex data/indices, `<map>.json` (surfaces, materials with every image and semantic hash: `a0ab1041` colour, `59d30d0f` normal, `389dd40f`/`199a03d3`... gloss and others; static models with origin, 3x3 axes rows, scale) and `<map>.ents` (the entity string).
- Greyhound (as for the DG-2, in BO3's Steam Runtime container, driven with xdotool; paste a comma-separated name list via `xclip -selection clipboard` into the search box: it matches substrings) exported the map's 444 textures, its 822 prop models (the search also catches their `i_mtl_*` textures) and 299 more prop textures found by reading each SEModel's materials. No `.gdt` was written; SEModels name their textures.
- `.tools/build_bo3_map.py` (`blender -b -P ... -- zm_factory the-giant`; Blender only for numpy) writes `mods/maps/the-giant/` (git-ignored): one GLB with the level split by material and 1024-unit cell, props as `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` nodes (per model per 2048-unit cell), a `COL_world` mesh (solid materials inside the zones' area + 900: CustomWorld's collision and navmesh use only `COL_` meshes) and markers from the entities: `info_player_start`; `spawn_location`/`riser_location` structs (zone from `<zone>_spawners`, `receiver` = start); `zbarrier_zmcore_basicwoodbarrier*` windows, each with an extra ZSPAWN 250 units toward its own spawner (same `script_string`; the real ones are farther than CustomWorld's 400); `zbarrier_zmcore_MagicBox` (`start_chest_zbarrier` = BOX_start); `zbarrier_zmcore_packapunch`; `use_power_switch`; `zm_perk_machine` structs (two on one spot: the later one slides 62 units to the left); `weapon_upgrade` structs mapped to this game's guns (M8A7 M16, KRM Stakeout, RK5 CZ75, Sheiva M14, Vesper MPL, VMP MP5K, HVK FAMAS, KN-44 AK117, L-CAR G18); `claymore_purchase`. Textures: WebP, external (`tex/`), 512 px level / 256 px props (1024 px made ~2 GB of VRAM). CoD inches; (x, y, z) -> (x, z, -y); CoD winding flipped [0, 2, 1].
- Map entry: `machines: "bo3"`, `perkYaw: -90` (markers carry BO3's own angles; machine GLBs face +Z), `nearbySpawns`. Loads in ~19 s, 72 FPS at the spawn, ~1,370 draws.
- **Doors**: each `zombie_door` trigger_use (two can share a `target`) -> a `DOOR_<zone>_<cost>` group of its `script_model` pieces (Greyhound-exported `p7_zm_der_door_buy_*`, `p7_zm_der_barricade_wall_main`) placed at their origin/yaw, each with extras `move` = BO3's `script_vector` (a world offset: the halves slide apart, the barricades drop 79). Zone from `script_flag` `enter_<zone>`; `electric_door` (10000) -> `electric: true`. The brush-model door parts (`*67`..`*78`) are skipped (their geometry isn't in the exported surface range; nothing stray at the origin). CustomWorld: a door piece with `userData.move` slides open over 1.1 s (ease-out, collision off at once) in `updateMovers` (custom.js now calls it even without movers), then hides; `setDoors` on reset puts the pieces back.
- **Zones**: CustomWorld reads a marker's zone up to the first underscore, so zone ids drop underscores (`outsidewest`) and markers carry `extras.zone`. BO3 zones without a door of their own open with their neighbours (`ZONE_GROUPS`: warehouse top/bottom -> `warehousebuilding`, wnuen + wnuen bridge -> `wnuenbuilding`, `bridge` -> both, `outside_south` -> outsideeast and outsidewest; spawners are placed once per listed zone). `receiver` is the start zone.
- **Teleporters**: `telepad_<n>` structs with `script_sound evt_pad` (the pad centres, z - 20) -> `TELEPORT_pad<n>` (cost 1500, cooldown 30, power) + `TPDEST_pad<n>` at the mainframe (-56, 440, 64 CoD, facing -Y): one-way pad -> mainframe, as in Der Riese. - **Linking and the Pack-a-Punch door** (`mods/the-giant`, active only on that map): the pads are built `locked` (CustomWorld teleporter property; the prompt shows `lockedText`). The build leaves empty nodes `GIANT_LEVER_pad<n>` (the `evt_lever_pull` structs) and `GIANT_CORE` (`trigger_teleport_core`) and a scripted `DOOR_pap_0` (the `pack_door` script_model, `move` 110 up). With the power on, a lever starts a 30 s link (announcement); the mainframe switch (shown only while a link is pending, with the countdown) links the pad (session flag `giant:linked:pad<n>`, `tp.locked = false`); a timeout fails it. All three: the door opens (`session.openDoors` + `setDoors`, so it rises) and `world.papLocked` clears. Reset relocks everything (flags are per session). Verified headless, including a timed-out link and a reset.
- **Generic custom-map hooks added for it**: TELEPORT `locked`/`lockedText`; DOOR `scripted` (no buy prompt; a script opens it); `world.papLocked`/`papLockedText` (custom.js shows it and refuses Pack-a-Punch).
- Verified headless: 9 doors (7 bought: prompt, cost, collision off, pieces moved 30-90 units, hidden; zones start -> each door's), both electric barricades open with the power, all three pads charge 1500 and land in the mainframe; Cheese Cube and Sandbox doors unchanged.
- **Look fixes (2026-10-07)**: Greyhound writes models in **centimetres**: every SEModel (props, doors, perk machines) is scaled by 1/2.54 (they were 2.54x too big). BO3 `_blend` materials and decals are overlays faded by the BSP vertex alpha (vertex RGB is white): they are `alphaMode BLEND` with COLOR_0 (uint8 normalized) and pushed 0.12 along the normal (no z-fighting); drawn opaque they smeared over the floors. Materials whose colour map is a built-in (`$white_diffuse`, `$gray_color`, `$color_black_NN`...; their real colour is a tint the SEModel lacks) get a colour from the material name (`TINTS`) instead of white. Textures: colour 1024, normal 512, props 512 (file names carry the size).
- **Lighting** (`mods/the-giant` `setupLook`): the engine's default lights are retuned every frame to a snowy night (ambient 0.55, 0.75 with power; hemisphere sky #a3b3c9 / ground #3a3129 0.85; moon key #c9d5ff 1.1; fill 0.25); map entry background #141a22, fog 0.00022, exposure 0.95. A fixed pool of 8 warm PointLights (range 650, decay 1.4; 700 before the power, 2200 after) follows the nearest of the `GIANT_LAMP_<n>` nodes (BO3's `lights`/`dyn_light`/`lantern` structs merged within 120), re-picked every 0.5 s. Uncapped 1600x900 at the spawn: 71 FPS, 1,343 draws (the 1,018 transparent overlays cost ~12 FPS).
- **Playtest fixes (2026-10-07)**: (1) Props had no collision (only `COL_world` = level geometry): the stairs (`p7_zm_der_stair_step_*`) and the teleporter floors are props, so you fell after teleporting and walked through steps.
