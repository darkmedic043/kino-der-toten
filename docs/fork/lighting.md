# Lighting mod (Kino and all maps)

- **Lighting mod** (`mods/lighting`, rewritten 2026-09-29). Kino: the 36 exported `light` entities plus fixture script_models become sources; high narrow spots (fov ≤ 60, y > 400) become spot sources (theatre balcony spots aim at the stage ≈ (0,90,−1100); the export lost their targets; lobby ones aim down). The theatre chandelier (radius 1700, drop 120) and `lights_hang_single` are downward spot fixtures. A pool of 12 PointLights follows the strongest sources; up to 4 shadow-casting SpotLights (High 2 at 1024², Ultra 4 at 2048²) follow the nearest spot sources. `renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate=false` with refresh at 30/60 Hz; never toggle light `.visible` (recompiles every shader); `castShadow` changes only with quality. Exposure 1.45, fill scaled to ambient .19 / hemi .21 / dir .12, darker fog and background. Spot sources get beams (see below) and drifting dust Points. Beams are volumetric (2026-09-29): each beam is an oversized cone (BackSide, 24 segments) in a separate `volScene`. `VolumetricPass` (right after RenderPass; the composer target has a DepthTexture) renders them at half resolution into its own target, then adds that onto the frame before AO and bloom. The shader marches the view ray (28 steps, interleaved-gradient jitter) from exit − (len + 2.5·rBot) to min(exit, scene depth). Density is Gaussian across the beam (exp(−q²·4.2)) × a fade along the axis × two-octave drifting value noise (dust). A forward-scatter phase mix(.7, 1.45, max(−rd·axis, 0)⁴) keeps looking into a light a soft glow, not a disc. There is no visible surface, so there are no edge lines or rims (the old cone surfaces and ribbons both showed artefacts or looked flat). Test on the real GTX 1660 Ti: headless Chromium with `--use-angle=vulkan --enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist`; SwiftShader hid the beam artefacts. To test on the real GTX 1660 Ti, launch headless Chromium with `--use-angle=vulkan --enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist`. With the power on, the chandelier materials get an emissive map of the candle-bulb block (top-left 20/128 of the texture). Directional bulbs (2026-09-29 later): the point pool became 12 soft SpotLights (penumbra 1); each non-spot source is oriented at load. Wall-mounted ones (a collision ray within 95 horizontally) move 30 off the wall and face out and down, so there's no hot circle. Ceiling ones face down; free ones (chandeliers) get a wide down cone. Perk machines (`PERKS`: Jugg red, Speed green, Double Tap amber, Quick Revive blue and always on, Pack-a-Punch purple) light the floor in front, picking the most open of 4 directions at y+78. Downward spot targets are raycast from 40 below the fixture (from the fixture itself, the ray hit the lamp body), and beams shorter than 60 are skipped. Beams fade out before they reach a surface (`smoothstep(0,.55,vH)`, length ×0.85), and bloom is gentler (.28, .32, .9). Daytime (`settings.daytime`, default on): a three.js `Sky` (vendored `objects/Sky.js`) that follows the camera, and a shadow-casting sun DirectionalLight over the map bounds (2048/4096 map, `shadow.autoUpdate=false`, refreshed every 2 s). The `sky_day` brushes (a 64 px placeholder filling roof holes and windows) are hidden in daytime. Day fog 0x9aa7b3 .00016, fill ambient .2 / hemi .3 / dir .08 (the user wanted unlit rooms properly dim), GTAO blend 1. Every light is capped by the nearest surface inside its cone (the axis plus a ring of rays 50° around it): cap = 3.2·near^1.3 (spots ×1.6; near < 2 means the probe started inside the fixture, so use 40), so no blown-out white discs. On top of that, a per-light shader cap: the lighting mod patches `ShaderChunk.lights_fragment_begin` so each point and spot light is scaled down to at most `LIGHT_CLAMP` (3.2) in its brightest channel. It is enabled per material by that define (set in `tagShadows`, which also forces the recompile), because lamps inside their own small fixture models (the stage-light prop at the lobby cable, floor-level fire lights) bloomed into white or cyan discs on a real GPU and the collision mesh cannot see those props. Low quality has no sun (without shadows it would light every interior). Beam dust: soft round motes 0.35 world units, opacity ≤ 0.2, count ≤ 110, random drift (on a real GPU the old 1.6-unit square points turned into rows and rings of glowing orbs; SwiftShader draws them tiny, so check point sizes by maths, not headless screenshots). Sun shafts: at load, grid-trace (32 u) walkable floors (collision) that the sun reaches and that have a roof overhead, testing against the visible opaque meshes with `three-mesh-bvh` (the collision mesh has no roofs). Flood-fill them into ≤28 shafts drawn with the beam builder `makeBeam` (5 shafts from 96 spots). Shadow refresh is per light (`shadow.needsUpdate`) with `renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate=true`. The HDR sanitise ShaderPass (NaN/Inf → 0, clamp 48) runs before AO and bloom, and AO temporarily hides transparent and additive objects (beams, glass), which fixed the black bars. Map fixtures (2026-09-29, later): `mods/lighting/fixtures.json` is baked from kino.gltf's `i_*` instance nodes: 6 chandeliers, 58 hanging lamps, 7 sconces, 151 mirror bulbs clustered to 20, 65 stage lamps (6 lit), street, overhead, tinhat and wall lights, and cage lights. Each becomes a source by kind (KIND table). Skipped: the fallen theatre chandelier, stage lamps lying on the floor, and sconces knocked askew. Sconces and wall lights are always on (they light the spawn lobby before power). Beams only come from sources at a visible fixture (no more beams from the theatre ceiling spots, which have none), and never from chandeliers. Chandeliers get candle halos: additive sprites at each candle bulb, found from vertices mapped to the bulb block of the chandelier texture (98 candles), flickering with power. Wall lights face the room: toward the centroid of lights within 750, falling back to the side with navmesh. Near-surface probes skip the fixture's own body (25) and cap = (near>40 ? 10 : 3.2)·near^1.3. Contrast: exposure 1.7, fill ambient .11 / hemi .17 / dir .05 by day (.12 / .14 / .08 at night), POOL 16, sun 3.4, bloom threshold 1.05, glass roughness .42 (glossier gave sun glints that bloomed into orbs). All maps: `host.renderWorld` runs an EffectComposer (4× MSAA HalfFloat target): RenderPass → GTAOPass (High+) → UnrealBloomPass (.38, .55, .82) → OutputPass. Settings → Graphics quality (`settings.graphics` 0–3, default 2; touch devices cap at 1). The add-ons are vendored from node_modules/three 0.185.1 into `vendor/jsm/{postprocessing,shaders,math}`.
- **Light pool (2026-09-29, later)**: the pool size follows quality (Low 8, Medium 10, High 14, Ultra 20; it is only resized then, because the count is in the shaders). **With the baked volume** (Kino) the pool is Low 5, Medium 6, High 8, Ultra 10 (2026-10-24; was 5/5/6/8) and holds only the dynamic sources: spot fixtures, fire, the box lamp. Handover: a slot fades its old light out, then the new one in; a waiting light only takes a slot from one it outscores ×1.4, and lights dropping out of the top n×1.5 fade out. Importance weighting (later): each pooled light is scaled by smoothstep(score; cutoff, 2.2·cutoff), where cutoff is the score of the first light outside the budget, so lights join and leave the pool nearly dark. Intensity changes are eased (1.1/s, handovers 2.5/s) and rate-limited to 900/s. On a navmesh walk from spawn to the power switch the largest per-frame change of any light went 672 → 54, and frames with a jump over 120 went 249 → 0.
- **Spot fixtures and the shadow spots (2026-10-24)**: Kino has 72 spot-type fixtures (the hanging lamps `lights_hang_single`, the chandelier), excluded from the bake because they are live lights. Before, on High/Ultra they were kept out of the pool and only the 1 (High) or 3 (Ultra) shadow-casting spots were live, reassigned to the top-scored fixtures every 0.25 s with no hysteresis and a 6/s snap to zero on a swap: walking a corridor, each lamp popped on as it became the nearest and off a few steps later (the user's report). Now every spot fixture not holding a shadow slot lights from the pool (with its fades and hysteresis), a fixture fading out of its shadow slot is free for the pool so the two crossfade, and the shadow slots keep a source until a candidate outscores it x1.4, handing over with a fade-out then fade-in (`userData.next`/`fadeOut`, eased 2.5/s, rate-limited 2500/s). Measured on a 256-frame walk under 14 lamps at High (`scratch/lights/walk.mjs`, not in git): largest per-frame intensity change 5182 → 48, frames with a jump over 120: 101 → 0, lit spot fixtures at a time 1.0 → 6.2, shadow-slot swaps 10 → 2.
- **Sky**: the Sky shader output is ×0.2 (it was blinding at exposure 1.7).
- **Viewmodel follows world light** (Kino, `lightTheGun` every 3 frames): it sums the live pool and spot lights reaching the camera (quadratic falloff, cone, and a cached collision ray for occlusion) plus 0.12 inside a sun shaft. The level is clamp(.22 + sum·8, .22, 1.3), smoothed, and scales the viewScene key, ambient, fill and rim; the key is tinted by the weighted light colour and aimed from the weighted light direction in camera space. Measured key: dark corridor 0.48, beside a sconce 2.86, sun shaft 2.6.
- **Baked lighting** (`mods/lighting/baked.js`; `.tools/bake-lighting.mjs` loads the page with `?bakeLight`, about 35 s–6 min on the GTX 1660 Ti): an irradiance volume, a 32-unit grid (154×68×180) over the lights' reach. Each cell holds RGB from always-on lamps, RGB from power lamps, and the dominant light direction, sqrt-encoded ×8 as uint8. It is stored as `baked-light.bin.gz` (1.5 MB; 17 MB raw) and inflated with DecompressionStream, plus `baked-light.json` (origin, cell, dims, scale). The bake uses the same falloff as the dynamic lights (decay 1.3, range falloff, spot smoothstep, per-light LIGHT_CLAMP) and occlusion rays against the visible opaque meshes (three-mesh-bvh), stopping 40 short of the bulb because lamps sit inside their own fixture model (stopping 12 short blocked nearly everything). Runtime: `patchMaterial` (applied in `tagShadows`, so zombies and props get it too) samples the volume at the world position, nudged 0.45 cell along the normal, and adds light × mix(.45, 1, N·dir) × bakedStrength .85 to `irradiance` after `lights_fragment_maps`; `bakedPowerMix` eases with the power. Only spot sources, fire, the box lamp, the box beam and the sun stay dynamic (`dynamicOnly`); the pool shrinks to 5/6/8 (Medium/High/Ultra). `lightTheGun` adds `volume.sample(camera)`. Why: 30 lamps within reach at once (50 within 1.5× range) is more than any dynamic pool, so lamps popped in; baked lamps are always on. Rebake whenever fixtures or sources change.
- **Mystery box lamp and beacon** (lighting mod): each `world.boxLocations` spot gets a green (#44ff6a) light source of kind 'box' placed under the ceiling above it (a collision ray up, ≤150), plus an additive halo sprite, lit only when the power is on and it is `world.activeBox`. The game's flat `world.boxBeam` cylinder is hidden and replaced by a volumetric beam (makeBeam, kind 'boxbeam', from the box upward, 1500 long, r 34, #a9c8ff) that follows `world.boxBeam.position` and is off during a fire sale or when the beam is removed.
- **Viewmodel lighting** (lighting mod, all maps): the viewScene had ambient 2.8 plus one light, which bleached every gun grey-white (the colour maps average about 80/255). Now ambient .6 (#f2e8da), key 2.2 (#fff0dc), hemisphere fill .7 and a neutral rim .7. Blue fill and rim tinted the guns blue.
- **Mystery box maps** (2026-09-30): each box location has a chalkboard map of the theatre behind it, with 9 `magic_box_loc_light` structs (`script_noteworthy` = `<location>_chest_loc`) that the export dropped. The lighting mod places a `zombie_zapper_cagelight` at each (its bulb faces local −Y), oriented by raycasting the *rendered* board from the room side (the boards aren't in collision). The bulb for `world.activeBox` glows green on every map, all glow in a fire sale, they flicker while the teddy carries the box away, and they're dim before power. Each has its own small halo. The box's green light source now sits at its map bulb (intensity 2.2, radius 240, facing out), replacing the old lamp that floated under the ceiling.

## Moon (`mods/lighting/moon.js`)

BO1 baked Moon's lighting into lightmaps we don't have, so Moon was lit only by a flat ambient + hemisphere + key light. The lighting mod now runs on Moon (it no longer has `skipMaps`) and calls `setupMoon` instead of `setupKino`. Bloom/AO and the viewmodel lights are shared.

- **Sources**: the lamp models in `moon.gltf` (instanced nodes `i_<model>_<n>`; table `LAMPS`: ceiling fixtures white/blue/yellow, tube lights white/red/green/yellow, light boxes, the yellow beam lamps, the cage light, the trap switch lights, and Area 51's floodlights marked `earth`) plus the 14 `light` entities (`light_off` = amber emergency lamps, power-gated; `fire_flicker` flickers). Lamps within 90 units of the same colour merge, giving 139 sources.
- **Baked volume** (`moon-light.json` + `moon-light.bin.gz`, 40-unit cells, made by `node .tools/bake-lighting.mjs moon`, which loads `play.html?map=moon&bakeLight`): all station lamps except Earth's and the flickering ones. Each lamp is baked as 55% solid + 45% power (`light_off` lamps are power only), so `bakedPowerMix = power` reproduces the live levels. With `?bakeLight` the Moon map module skips the instancing optimizer and culler, because the bake's BVH rays can't see instanced batches. **Rebake when `LAMPS`, the light entities or the source merging change.**
- **Pool**: down-aimed wide `SpotLight`s, reassigned every 0.25 s to the highest-scoring live sources on the player's side (moon vs earth), with fade in/out. With the volume that's 3/3/4/6 by quality (only Earth floodlights and flickering lamps); without it, 4/6/8/12. Never toggle `.visible`.
- **Performance** (1600×900 headless, 1660 Ti, Receiving Bay): no lighting mod 57 fps; first version (14 live lights, sun shadow redrawn every 0.4 s) 27 fps; separately, the pool cost 10–20 fps, the sun shadow and post 5–10 each.
- **Sun**: from worldspawn `sundirection "-16.28 56.06 0"` (16° up; BO1 (x,y,z) → three (x,z,-y)) and `suncolor`. It is a hard, low light with a ±2900 shadow map that follows the camera in 500-unit steps and redraws only on such a step (Ultra also every 0.35 s, for actor shadows). Meshes under 30 units don't cast it. Intensity is 3.2 on the Moon and 1.1 on Earth. Glass (`/glass|window_warehouse/`) and sky never cast shadows: the biodome dome blocked the sun until it was excluded.
- **Fill**: ambient ×0.3 and hemi ×0.28 of the map's values on the Moon (×0.45 / ×0.42 on Earth). Power raises fixtures from 55% to 100% and the fill by 25%.
- **Glow**: lamp materials (`GLOWING`) take their diffuse map as an emissive map, so bloom picks them up.
- **Map state**: read through `api.getState().moon` four times a second (the snapshot is too heavy to read every frame).

- **Viewmodel light on other maps (2026-10-16)**: the lighting mod sets the first-person lights for Kino's exposure (1.7). Other maps keep their own exposure (Sandbox, The Giant 0.95), which left guns and arms near black (user: "lack of a global light"); on every map but Kino and Moon the viewmodel lights are scaled by 1.7 / exposure each frame.

- **Colour grade and film look (2026-10-22, `mods/lighting/grade.js`)**: the composer's OutputPass is replaced by `GradePass`, which does the same tone mapping (the renderer's, ACES at its exposure) and sRGB conversion with a BO3/BO7-style grade in between: chromatic aberration (radial) → unsharp mask on the clamped HDR frame → tone map → white balance → split toning (cool shadows, warm highlights, weighted by luminance) → vibrance/saturation → vignette → sRGB → crushed blacks → cubic S-curve around mid grey (keeps 0 and 1; its shadow half pulls at 70%, since at full strength the Theatre and Dining Hall lost their detail, 2026-10-22) → grain (heavier in shadows). The old sanitise ShaderPass became the `HAZE_SHADER` pass: same NaN/Inf clean-up plus depth haze (aerial perspective) in HDR, before AO and bloom; it is the chain's first swap pass so its read buffer always holds the scene's depth (the composer alternates targets each frame). `ViewmodelPass` draws the first-person weapon into the frame after the AO (the gun's depth is in the view camera's projection, which the AO must not read) and before bloom and the grade, so the gun is graded, bloomed and vignetted with the world: the engine passes `{viewScene,viewCamera,drawView}` to `mods.renderWorld` and skips its own viewmodel draw when the hook returns true; the warm-up records the target per scene, so the gun's shaders compile for the HDR target while loading. Presets per map in `GRADES` (merged over `default`, then a map entry's `grade` object in maps.json); Settings → Graphics → **Film look** (Off/Light/Full; Light is every knob at half strength) and **Film grain**. Live tuning in a game: `kino.post.set({contrast:1.3})`, `kino.post.reset()`. Low graphics has no composer, so no grade. Bloom on the gun: the view scene's lights are bright (ambient 2.8), so watch the bloom threshold (1.05) against pale weapon textures.

- **Fire (2026-10-27, `mods/fx/flames.js`, user: "make the fire look like actual fire")**: every map's fixtures used to draw one smooth teardrop point sprite per flame (the castle's `weather.js` shader, copied by Nuketown's `atmosphere.js`), additive and bloomed to a white blob: a candle everywhere, and Kino's three `fire_flicker` lights had no flame at all. `createFlames({scene, renderer, low, name, fade})` draws four instanced billboard layers, one draw call each, view-aligned quads anchored at the fixture point (no point-sprite size cap, no popping at the screen edge): **flames** (a teardrop body eaten from the top by scrolling 3-octave value noise, so tongues form, stretch and tear off; dark red edge → orange → yellow → a white core scaled by `hot`; candles keep a blue base; `sub` overlapping sub-flames of different heights and rates per fire; a far flame stays at least 4 px tall and dims by 1/k² so it doesn't shimmer), **embers** (sparks rising on a turbulent path, 2-6 px, twinkling out; `float: true` = embers that hang and drift with no fire under them, Nuketown's emb1 emitters), **smoke** (noise-broken puffs rising and spreading, warm underneath while young, normal blending, mixed to the scene fog, lit by the `flash` uniform in lightning) and **glow** (a soft additive halo breathing with the flicker, gone within 110 units so it never covers the flame up close). `KINDS` = candle (9 units), lamp (12, cooler, behind glass), torch (34, 3 subs, embers and smoke), fire (60, 5 subs, 14 embers, 6 puffs); `add(p, kind, opts)` overrides any field, `build()` fills the buffers (callable after async data; it clears three's `_maxInstanceCount`, which the first draw pins at the stub's count, or only one instance ever draws), `update(dt, {fog, flash})`. Tuning uniforms (`fire.uniforms`, live): `gain` .55 (brightness; 1 bloomed every fire to white), `core` .35 (white-core share), `alpha` .9, `glowK` .5. `fireFlicker(t, seed)` (0.68-1.0, incommensurate sines: a slow breathing, a 9 Hz waver, fast jitter) is the one flicker curve: the flames use it in GLSL, and the fire lights use it on the CPU with the flame's seed (Kino's `level()`, the castle's lamp pool, Nuketown's fire pool), so a light pulses with its flame. Low quality: 2 subs at most, no smoke, half the embers. Wiring: Kino (`setupKino`) adds a `fire` for every `fire_flicker` light source, placed on the top surface under it by a ray cast down from 120 above (the lobby's light sits inside its debris pile, so a short ray found the floor under the pile and buried the flame; `s.fireSize`, default 64) and keeps it in `s.flame`; the castle (`weather.js`, `kino.castle.weather.fire`) adds one per `CASTLE_FLAME_*` node of its `userData.kind`/`size`; Nuketown (`kino.nukeAtmo.fire`) maps its fx kinds (`KINDS` in atmosphere.js) onto the module's. Stub instances sit far below the world so the programs compile in the load-time warm-up. Checked on the GPU with `scratch`-style capture scripts; the uniforms were picked from gain 1 / .7 / .5 side by side (`.5` reads as fire, the others as a bloomed lamp).
