# Lighting mod (Kino and all maps)

- **Lighting mod** (`mods/lighting`, rewritten 2026-09-29). Kino: the 36 exported `light` entities plus fixture script_models become sources; high narrow spots (fov ≤ 60, y > 400) become spot sources (theatre balcony spots aim at the stage ≈ (0,90,−1100); the export lost their targets; lobby ones aim down). The theatre chandelier (radius 1700, drop 120) and `lights_hang_single` are downward spot fixtures. A pool of 12 PointLights follows the strongest sources; up to 4 shadow-casting SpotLights (High 2 at 1024², Ultra 4 at 2048²) follow the nearest spot sources. `renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate=false` with refresh at 30/60 Hz; never toggle light `.visible` (recompiles every shader); `castShadow` changes only with quality. Exposure 1.45, fill scaled to ambient .19 / hemi .21 / dir .12, darker fog and background. Spot sources get beams (see below) and drifting dust Points. Beams are volumetric (2026-09-29): each beam is an oversized cone (BackSide, 24 segments) in a separate `volScene`. `VolumetricPass` (right after RenderPass; the composer target has a DepthTexture) renders them at half resolution into its own target, then adds that onto the frame before AO and bloom. The shader marches the view ray (28 steps, interleaved-gradient jitter) from exit − (len + 2.5·rBot) to min(exit, scene depth). Density is Gaussian across the beam (exp(−q²·4.2)) × a fade along the axis × two-octave drifting value noise (dust). A forward-scatter phase mix(.7, 1.45, max(−rd·axis, 0)⁴) keeps looking into a light a soft glow, not a disc. There is no visible surface, so there are no edge lines or rims (the old cone surfaces and ribbons both showed artefacts or looked flat). Test on the real GTX 1660 Ti: headless Chromium with `--use-angle=vulkan --enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist`; SwiftShader hid the beam artefacts. To test on the real GTX 1660 Ti, launch headless Chromium with `--use-angle=vulkan --enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist`. With the power on, the chandelier materials get an emissive map of the candle-bulb block (top-left 20/128 of the texture). Directional bulbs (2026-09-29 later): the point pool became 12 soft SpotLights (penumbra 1); each non-spot source is oriented at load. Wall-mounted ones (a collision ray within 95 horizontally) move 30 off the wall and face out and down, so there's no hot circle. Ceiling ones face down; free ones (chandeliers) get a wide down cone. Perk machines (`PERKS`: Jugg red, Speed green, Double Tap amber, Quick Revive blue and always on, Pack-a-Punch purple) light the floor in front, picking the most open of 4 directions at y+78. Downward spot targets are raycast from 40 below the fixture (from the fixture itself, the ray hit the lamp body), and beams shorter than 60 are skipped. Beams fade out before they reach a surface (`smoothstep(0,.55,vH)`, length ×0.85), and bloom is gentler (.28, .32, .9). Daytime (`settings.daytime`, default on): a three.js `Sky` (vendored `objects/Sky.js`) that follows the camera, and a shadow-casting sun DirectionalLight over the map bounds (2048/4096 map, `shadow.autoUpdate=false`, refreshed every 2 s). The `sky_day` brushes (a 64 px placeholder filling roof holes and windows) are hidden in daytime. Day fog 0x9aa7b3 .00016, fill ambient .2 / hemi .3 / dir .08 (the user wanted unlit rooms properly dim), GTAO blend 1. Every light is capped by the nearest surface inside its cone (the axis plus a ring of rays 50° around it): cap = 3.2·near^1.3 (spots ×1.6; near < 2 means the probe started inside the fixture, so use 40), so no blown-out white discs. On top of that, a per-light shader cap: the lighting mod patches `ShaderChunk.lights_fragment_begin` so each point and spot light is scaled down to at most `LIGHT_CLAMP` (3.2) in its brightest channel. It is enabled per material by that define (set in `tagShadows`, which also forces the recompile), because lamps inside their own small fixture models (the stage-light prop at the lobby cable, floor-level fire lights) bloomed into white or cyan discs on a real GPU and the collision mesh cannot see those props. Low quality has no sun (without shadows it would light every interior). Beam dust: soft round motes 0.35 world units, opacity ≤ 0.2, count ≤ 110, random drift (on a real GPU the old 1.6-unit square points turned into rows and rings of glowing orbs; SwiftShader draws them tiny, so check point sizes by maths, not headless screenshots). Sun shafts: at load, grid-trace (32 u) walkable floors (collision) that the sun reaches and that have a roof overhead, testing against the visible opaque meshes with `three-mesh-bvh` (the collision mesh has no roofs). Flood-fill them into ≤28 shafts drawn with the beam builder `makeBeam` (5 shafts from 96 spots). Shadow refresh is per light (`shadow.needsUpdate`) with `renderer.shadowMap.autoUpdate=true`. The HDR sanitise ShaderPass (NaN/Inf → 0, clamp 48) runs before AO and bloom, and AO temporarily hides transparent and additive objects (beams, glass), which fixed the black bars. Map fixtures (2026-09-29, later): `mods/lighting/fixtures.json` is baked from kino.gltf's `i_*` instance nodes: 6 chandeliers, 58 hanging lamps, 7 sconces, 151 mirror bulbs clustered to 20, 65 stage lamps (6 lit), street, overhead, tinhat and wall lights, and cage lights. Each becomes a source by kind (KIND table). Skipped: the fallen theatre chandelier, stage lamps lying on the floor, and sconces knocked askew. Sconces and wall lights are always on (they light the spawn lobby before power). Beams only come from sources at a visible fixture (no more beams from the theatre ceiling spots, which have none), and never from chandeliers. Chandeliers get candle halos: additive sprites at each candle bulb, found from vertices mapped to the bulb block of the chandelier texture (98 candles), flickering with power. Wall lights face the room: toward the centroid of lights within 750, falling back to the side with navmesh. Near-surface probes skip the fixture's own body (25) and cap = (near>40 ? 10 : 3.2)·near^1.3. Contrast: exposure 1.7, fill ambient .11 / hemi .17 / dir .05 by day (.12 / .14 / .08 at night), POOL 16, sun 3.4, bloom threshold 1.05, glass roughness .42 (glossier gave sun glints that bloomed into orbs). All maps: `host.renderWorld` runs an EffectComposer (4× MSAA HalfFloat target): RenderPass → GTAOPass (High+) → UnrealBloomPass (.38, .55, .82) → OutputPass. Settings → Graphics quality (`settings.graphics` 0–3, default 2; touch devices cap at 1). The add-ons are vendored from node_modules/three 0.185.1 into `vendor/jsm/{postprocessing,shaders,math}`.
- **Light pool (2026-09-29, later)**: the pool size follows quality (Low 8, Medium 10, High 14, Ultra 20; it is only resized then, because the count is in the shaders). **With the baked volume** (Kino) the pool is Low 5, Medium 6, High 8, Ultra 10 (2026-10-24; was 5/5/6/8) and holds only the dynamic sources: spot fixtures, fire, the box lamp. Handover: a slot fades its old light out, then the new one in; a waiting light only takes a slot from one it outscores ×1.4, and lights dropping out of the top n×1.5 fade out. Importance weighting (later): each pooled light is scaled by smoothstep(score; cutoff, 2.2·cutoff), where cutoff is the score of the first light outside the budget, so lights join and leave the pool nearly dark. Intensity changes are eased (1.1/s, handovers 2.5/s) and rate-limited to 900/s. On a navmesh walk from spawn to the power switch the largest per-frame change of any light went 672 → 54, and frames with a jump over 120 went 249 → 0.
- **Spot fixtures and the shadow spots (2026-10-24)**: Kino has 72 spot-type fixtures (the hanging lamps `lights_hang_single`, the chandelier), excluded from the bake because they are live lights. Before, on High/Ultra they were kept out of the pool and only the 1 (High) or 3 (Ultra) shadow-casting spots were live, reassigned to the top-scored fixtures every 0.25 s with no hysteresis and a 6/s snap to zero on a swap: walking a corridor, each lamp popped on as it became the nearest and off a few steps later (the user's report). Now every spot fixture not holding a shadow slot lights from the pool (with its fades and hysteresis), a fixture fading out of its shadow slot is free for the pool so the two crossfade, and the shadow slots keep a source until a candidate outscores it x1.4, handing over with a fade-out then fade-in (`userData.next`/`fadeOut`, eased 2.5/s, rate-limited 2500/s). Measured on a 256-frame walk under 14 lamps at High (`scratch/lights/walk.mjs`, not in git): largest per-frame intensity change 5182 → 48, frames with a jump over 120: 101 → 0, lit spot fixtures at a time 1.0 → 6.2, shadow-slot swaps 10 → 2.
- **Shared beam cones** (2026-10-27): `beamCone(top, dir, len, r, color, {tip, warmth})` builds a volumetric cone in `volScene` and is exposed as `window.kino.volumeBeam` for other maps' lights (Nuketown's shafts, `mods/nuketown/atmosphere.js`). Kino's `makeBeam` uses it and adds the dust. The caller drives `uniforms.strength`/`time` and the mesh's visibility. A cone seen from inside blows out (the view ray runs down its whole length), so a caller whose beams the player can stand in fades them near the axis.
- **Sky**: the Sky shader output is ×0.2 (it was blinding at exposure 1.7).
- **Viewmodel follows world light** (Kino, `lightTheGun` every 3 frames): it sums the live pool and spot lights reaching the camera (quadratic falloff, cone, and a cached collision ray for occlusion) plus 0.12 inside a sun shaft. The level is clamp(.22 + sum·8, .22, 1.3), smoothed, and scales the viewScene key, ambient, fill and rim; the key is tinted by the weighted light colour and aimed from the weighted light direction in camera space. Measured key: dark corridor 0.48, beside a sconce 2.86, sun shaft 2.6.
- **Baked lighting** (`mods/lighting/baked.js`; `.tools/bake-lighting.mjs` loads the page with `?bakeLight`, about 35 s–6 min on the GTX 1660 Ti): an irradiance volume, a 32-unit grid (154×68×180) over the lights' reach. Each cell holds RGB from always-on lamps, RGB from power lamps, and the dominant light direction, sqrt-encoded ×8 as uint8. It is stored as `baked-light.bin.gz` (1.5 MB; 17 MB raw) and inflated with DecompressionStream, plus `baked-light.json` (origin, cell, dims, scale). The bake uses the same falloff as the dynamic lights (decay 1.3, range falloff, spot smoothstep, per-light LIGHT_CLAMP) and occlusion rays against the visible opaque meshes (three-mesh-bvh), stopping 40 short of the bulb because lamps sit inside their own fixture model (stopping 12 short blocked nearly everything). Runtime: `patchMaterial` (applied in `tagShadows`, so zombies and props get it too) samples the volume at the world position, nudged 0.45 cell along the normal, and adds light × mix(.45, 1, N·dir) × bakedStrength .85 to `irradiance` after `lights_fragment_maps`; `bakedPowerMix` eases with the power. Only spot sources, fire, the box lamp, the box beam and the sun stay dynamic (`dynamicOnly`); the pool shrinks to 5/6/8 (Medium/High/Ultra). `lightTheGun` adds `volume.sample(camera)`. Why: 30 lamps within reach at once (50 within 1.5× range) is more than any dynamic pool, so lamps popped in; baked lamps are always on. Rebake whenever fixtures or sources change.
- **Mystery box lamp and beacon** (lighting mod): each `world.boxLocations` spot gets a green (#44ff6a) light source of kind 'box' placed under the ceiling above it (a collision ray up, ≤150), plus an additive halo sprite, lit only when the power is on and it is `world.activeBox`. The game's flat `world.boxBeam` cylinder is hidden and replaced by a volumetric beam (makeBeam, kind 'boxbeam', from the box upward, 1500 long, r 34, #a9c8ff) that follows `world.boxBeam.position` and is off during a fire sale or when the beam is removed.
- **Viewmodel lighting** (lighting mod, all maps): the viewScene had ambient 2.8 plus one light, which bleached every gun grey-white (the colour maps average about 80/255). Now ambient .6 (#f2e8da), key 2.2 (#fff0dc), hemisphere fill .7 and a neutral rim .7. Blue fill and rim tinted the guns blue.
- **Mystery box maps** (2026-09-30): each box location has a chalkboard map of the theatre behind it, with 9 `magic_box_loc_light` structs (`script_noteworthy` = `<location>_chest_loc`) that the export dropped. The lighting mod places a `zombie_zapper_cagelight` at each (its bulb faces local −Y), oriented by raycasting the *rendered* board from the room side (the boards aren't in collision). The bulb for `world.activeBox` glows green on every map, all glow in a fire sale, they flicker while the teddy carries the box away, and they're dim before power. Each has its own small halo. The box's green light source now sits at its map bulb (intensity 2.2, radius 240, facing out), replacing the old lamp that floated under the ceiling.

## Moon (`mods/lighting/moon.js`)

BO1 baked Moon's lighting into lightmaps we don't have, so Moon was lit only by a flat ambient + hemisphere + key light. The lighting mod now runs on Moon (it no longer has `skipMaps`) and calls `setupMoon` instead of `setupKino`. Bloom/AO and the viewmodel lights are shared.

- **Sources**: the lamp models in `moon.gltf` (instanced nodes `i_<model>_<n>`; table `LAMPS`: ceiling fixtures white/blue/yellow, tube lights white/red/green/yellow, light boxes, the yellow beam lamps, the cage light, the trap switch lights, and Area 51's floodlights marked `earth`) plus the 14 `light` entities (`light_off` = amber emergency lamps, power-gated; `fire_flicker` flickers). Lamps within 90 units of the same colour merge, giving 139 sources.
- **Baked volume** (`moon-light.json` + `moon-light.bin.gz`, 40-unit cells, made by `node .tools/bake-lighting.mjs moon`, which loads `play.html?map=moon&bakeLight`): all station lamps except Earth's and the flickering ones. Each lamp is baked as 55% solid + 45% power (`light_off` lamps are power only), so `bakedPowerMix = power` reproduces the live levels. With `?bakeLight` the Moon map module skips the instancing optimizer and culler, because the bake's BVH rays can't see instanced batches. **Rebake when `LAMPS`, the light entities or the source merging change.**
- **Pool**: down-aimed wide `SpotLight`s, reassigned every 0.25 s to the highest-scoring live sources on the player's side (moon vs earth), with fade in/out. With the volume that's 3/3/4/6 by quality (only Earth floodlights and flickering lamps); without it, 4/6/8/12. Never toggle `.visible`.
- **Performance** (1600×900 headless, 1660 Ti, Receiving Bay): no lighting mod 57 fps; first version (14 live lights, sun shadow redrawn every 0.4 s) 27 fps; separately, the pool cost 10–20 fps, the sun shadow and post 5–10 each.
- **Sun**: from worldspawn `sundirection "-16.28 56.06 0"` (16° up; BO1 (x,y,z) → three (x,z,-y)) and `suncolor`. It is a hard, low light with a ±2900 shadow map that follows the camera in 500-unit steps and redraws only on such a step (Ultra also every 0.35 s, for actor shadows). Meshes under 30 units don't cast it. Intensity is 3.2 on the Moon and 1.1 on Earth. Glass (`/glass|window_warehouse/`) and sky never cast shadows: the biodome dome blocked the sun until it was excluded.
- **Fill**: ambient ×0.3 and hemi ×0.28 of the map's values on the Moon (×0.45 / ×0.42 on Earth). Power raises fixtures from 55% to 100% and the fill by 25%.
- **Glow**: lamp materials (`GLOWING`) take their diffuse map as an emissive map, so bloom picks them up.
- **Map state**: read through `api.getState().moon` four times a second (the snapshot is too heavy to read every frame).

- **Viewmodel light follows the map's light (2026-10-29, user: "its darker in nuketown, yet the fpv glows super bright")**: on every map but Kino and Moon the first-person lights are also scaled by the map's irradiance at the camera over the engine's default lights (`REF` 2.9): the scene's ambient and hemisphere, its directional lights at half (a shadow-casting sun only while a ray from the camera reaches it, one ray every 8 frames, smoothed), and the point and spot lights in range (intensity x luminance x range falloff / distance^decay, spots at 0.35). The ratio r = E/REF becomes the gain as r^1.6 below 1 (r above 1 as is), clamped to 0.08-1.4 and eased at 4/s (the linear ratio with a .25 floor still left Nuketown's hands three times brighter than the street; the user's gameplay video has them as dark as the road, which r≈.3-.47 → .15-.3 gives); `kino.vmLight` shows `{gain,target,sunVis}`. The lights list is re-collected from the scene every 120 frames (pools move). Nuketown's sky look (ambient 1.0 + hemi 1.25 + key 1.1) gives r about 0.3-0.5 on the street (gain .15-.3), more by a lamp or fire. `scratch/nukeref/cap.mjs` + `reload.mjs` (a held page over CDP, `scratch/nuke/hold.mjs`) capture the check frames. Kino keeps its baked volume look and Moon its own setup.
- **Viewmodel light on other maps (2026-10-16)**: the lighting mod sets the first-person lights for Kino's exposure (1.7). Other maps keep their own exposure (Sandbox, The Giant 0.95), which left guns and arms near black (user: "lack of a global light"); on every map but Kino and Moon the viewmodel lights are scaled by 1.7 / exposure each frame.

- **Colour grade and film look (2026-10-22, `mods/lighting/grade.js`)**: the composer's OutputPass is replaced by `GradePass`, which does the same tone mapping (the renderer's, ACES at its exposure) and sRGB conversion with a BO3/BO7-style grade in between: chromatic aberration (radial) → unsharp mask on the clamped HDR frame → tone map → white balance → split toning (cool shadows, warm highlights, weighted by luminance) → vibrance/saturation → vignette → sRGB → crushed blacks → cubic S-curve around mid grey (keeps 0 and 1; its shadow half pulls at 70%, since at full strength the Theatre and Dining Hall lost their detail, 2026-10-22) → grain (heavier in shadows). The old sanitise ShaderPass became the `HAZE_SHADER` pass: same NaN/Inf clean-up plus depth haze (aerial perspective) in HDR, before AO and bloom; it is the chain's first swap pass so its read buffer always holds the scene's depth (the composer alternates targets each frame). `ViewmodelPass` draws the first-person weapon into the frame after the AO (the gun's depth is in the view camera's projection, which the AO must not read) and before bloom and the grade, so the gun is graded, bloomed and vignetted with the world: the engine passes `{viewScene,viewCamera,drawView}` to `mods.renderWorld` and skips its own viewmodel draw when the hook returns true; the warm-up records the target per scene, so the gun's shaders compile for the HDR target while loading. Presets per map in `GRADES` (merged over `default`, then a map entry's `grade` object in maps.json); Settings → Graphics → **Film look** (Off/Light/Full; Light is every knob at half strength) and **Film grain**. Live tuning in a game: `kino.post.set({contrast:1.3})`, `kino.post.reset()`. Low graphics has no composer, so no grade. Bloom on the gun: the view scene's lights are bright (ambient 2.8), so watch the bloom threshold (1.05) against pale weapon textures. Transparent effects that write no depth get the haze of whatever is behind them (the sky's, over a near column): give a big one a depth-only twin drawn last, as Nuketown's fire tornadoes do (waw-ports.md Fx, 2026-11-04).
- **A map's own colour cube (2026-11-15, Gorod Krovi)**: the grade key `lut` (0-1, scaled by Settings → Film look like every other knob) blends the display-space picture through a 32³ colour cube, `lut.png` in the map's folder (a 1024x32 strip: 32 tiles of blue, red across a tile, green down it; `loadLut` and `GradePass.setLut` in `grade.js`). `setupPost` loads it into `host.pending` only when the map's preset has `lut`; without the file the mix stays 0. It is applied right after the sRGB conversion, before `black` and `contrast`: keep those neutral on a map with a cube, or the curve is applied twice. `.tools/bo3_ff_lut.py` writes the strip from a BO3 map's fastfile (game data: git-ignored map folder only). Live: `kino.post.set({lut:.5})`. The two fastfiles read so far (zm_stalingrad, zm_castle) hold the same cube, a plain tone curve with no tint.

- **Fire (2026-10-27, `mods/fx/flames.js`, user: "make the fire look like actual fire")**: every map's fixtures used to draw one smooth teardrop point sprite per flame (the castle's `weather.js` shader, copied by Nuketown's `atmosphere.js`), additive and bloomed to a white blob: a candle everywhere, and Kino's three `fire_flicker` lights had no flame at all. `createFlames({scene, renderer, low, name, fade})` draws four instanced billboard layers, one draw call each, view-aligned quads anchored at the fixture point (no point-sprite size cap, no popping at the screen edge): **flames** (a teardrop body eaten from the top by scrolling 3-octave value noise, so tongues form, stretch and tear off; dark red edge → orange → yellow → a white core scaled by `hot`; candles keep a blue base; `sub` overlapping sub-flames of different heights and rates per fire; a far flame stays at least 4 px tall and dims by 1/k² so it doesn't shimmer), **embers** (sparks rising on a turbulent path, 2-6 px, twinkling out; `float: true` = embers that hang and drift with no fire under them, Nuketown's emb1 emitters), **smoke** (noise-broken puffs rising and spreading, warm underneath while young, normal blending, mixed to the scene fog, lit by the `flash` uniform in lightning) and **glow** (a soft additive halo breathing with the flicker, gone within 110 units so it never covers the flame up close). `KINDS` = candle (9 units), lamp (12, cooler, behind glass), torch (34, 3 subs, embers and smoke), fire (60, 5 subs, 14 embers, 6 puffs); `add(p, kind, opts)` overrides any field, `build()` fills the buffers (callable after async data; it clears three's `_maxInstanceCount`, which the first draw pins at the stub's count, or only one instance ever draws), `update(dt, {fog, flash})`. Tuning uniforms (`fire.uniforms`, live): `gain` .55 (brightness; 1 bloomed every fire to white), `core` .35 (white-core share), `alpha` .9, `glowK` .5. `fireFlicker(t, seed)` (0.68-1.0, incommensurate sines: a slow breathing, a 9 Hz waver, fast jitter) is the one flicker curve: the flames use it in GLSL, and the fire lights use it on the CPU with the flame's seed (Kino's `level()`, the castle's lamp pool, Nuketown's fire pool), so a light pulses with its flame. Low quality: 2 subs at most, no smoke, half the embers. Wiring: The flame quads are axial billboards (standing along world up, turned about it to face the camera; they lean back toward the screen only when seen almost straight down): a screen-aligned quad seen from above lay across the shelf behind its candle. Kino (`setupKino`) adds a `fire` for every `fire_flicker` light source, placed on the top surface under it by a ray cast down from 120 above (the lobby's light sits inside its debris pile, so a short ray found the floor under the pile and buried the flame; `s.fireSize`, default 64) and keeps it in `s.flame`; the castle (`weather.js`, `kino.castle.weather.fire`) adds one per `CASTLE_FLAME_*` node of its `userData.kind`/`size`; Nuketown (`kino.nukeAtmo.fire`) maps its fx kinds (`KINDS` in atmosphere.js) onto the module's. Stub instances sit far below the world so the programs compile in the load-time warm-up. Checked on the GPU with `scratch`-style capture scripts; the uniforms were picked from gain 1 / .7 / .5 side by side (`.5` reads as fire, the others as a bloomed lamp). **Realism pass (2026-10-27, user: "make the fire more realistic")**: the flame body is a teardrop (half-width .78 of the quad at the base, .06 at the tip) with a breathing height (flicker), domain-warped fbm turbulence scrolling up and a second finer octave, eroded harder toward the tip (`erode`, 1.15) so tongues pinch off; the colour ramp is by depth into the tongue (`dd`): dark red skin, orange, yellow only past .42, a white core (`core`, .25) only low in the flame where it's hottest, and the tips never pass orange. The body is **premultiplied cover blending** (`One, OneMinusSrcAlpha`), colour × opacity in rgb and `opacity × soot` (.75) in alpha, so the 3-5 overlapping sub-flames of a fire cover each other instead of summing to white, which was the earlier blow-out; the erosion is masked by the body and the quad top, or low noise makes the whole quad a pale box (seen at close range). Sub-flames spread .08-.3 of the size; glow halo `glowK` .4. **Heat shimmer**: every fire with sub-flames and size ≥ 16 (torches and fires, not candles or lamps; `opts.heat:false` opts out) adds an instanced sheet (1.5 × size wide, 2.4 × size tall, axial billboard) to `kino.heatScene`, a scene the fire module never draws itself: the lighting mod renders it with the frame's camera into a half-size HalfFloat offset map (`heatRT`) inside its haze pass (`renderHeat`, mod.js) and `HAZE_SHADER` reads tDiffuse and tDepth at `vUv + tHeat.rg × heatK` (grade.js). The sheet's fragment outputs a distortion vector (the fbm gradient, a smooth swirl scrolling up, shaped to fade at the sides and top) scaled by `vAmp = min(.012, .03 × size / distance)` in uv, fading out with the module's `fade`; depth-occluded per pixel (shared uniforms `kino.heat` = tDepth/near/far/res), so a fire behind a wall doesn't bend it. Skipped when no fire has heat instances (`heatK` 0) and on Low (no composer). Checked on the GPU: Nuketown close/mid car fire and torches (quality 3), the Kino lobby fire, the castle at quality 1 (376 fires, no errors).

## Baked light volume for the BO3 ports (2026-11-12)
The Giant and Der Eisendrache lit their lamps with a pool of point lights that followed the camera (8 / 4-16 lights for 50 / 332 lamps), so rooms lit up and went dark as the pool moved, and interiors got the full sky ambient and moonlight through their roofs (user, 2026-11-11: "lighting popping in and out"). Both now read a baked volume.
- **Bake**: `node .tools/bake-map-light.mjs <the-giant|der-eisendrache> [--cell N]` (Node only, no browser; a few minutes). Reads the map's GLB, writes `light.json` + `light.bin.gz` next to it (git-ignored like the map: **rerun after every map rebuild** or after changing a number in its `MAPS` table, which must match the map mod's `LAMP` table). Grid cells of 40 (Giant, 140x39x167) / 48 (DE) units over the space within reach of the gameplay markers; per cell: lamp light with the power off and the extra with it on (walls block it, collision meshes are the occluders; DE adds `PCOL_props` and `COL_flagstones`), the dominant lamp direction, how much sky the cell sees (32 rays) and how much of the sun/moon reaches it. Cells inside solids (over 40% of the sky rays hit back faces) are left out. Lamp light then bounces: ten diffusion steps through open cell links, added at .45, so a lamp also lifts what it can't see directly; the sky value is smoothed and spread indoors from doorways and windows (1/e in about three cells).
- **Fill lights**: BO3 lights its rooms with many lights that have no model, so they are not among the lamp markers and whole rooms had no lamp light. Indoor standing-height cells with no lamp in sight get an invisible ceiling light in the bake (the most open spots first; reach 400, half a lamp, a little cooler). The Giant: 100 fills on 50 lamps; Der Eisendrache: 124 on 332. They exist only in the bake.
- **Runtime**: `loadMapLight(api, { skyFloor, indoor, sunFloor, strength, powerOff })` (`mods/lighting/map-volume.js`), awaited at the end of the map mod's setup (see the load-order note below), returns null without the files or with `?noBake` (the old pool then runs). Three RGBA 3D textures (lamp off + sun, lamp on + sky, direction); every lit material is patched once (`userData.mapLight`): ambient + hemisphere are scaled by the sky value down to `skyFloor` times the warm `indoor` tint, directional lights by the sun value down to `sunFloor`, and the lamp light is added. `powerOff` is the share of the power-on light shown before the power is on (Giant .3, DE .7: the rooms must stay readable); the power eases in over about a second. The map keeps tuning its own lights (colour, level, power): the volume only says where they reach. Outside the volume nothing changes.

## Der Eisendrache from the game's own lights (2026-11-12)

- **Source**: BO3 keeps a map's primary lights in its fastfile. `python3 -I .tools/bo3_ff_lights.py "<BO3>/zone/zm_castle.ff" export_game/bo3/maps/zm_castle/zm_castle.lights.json` writes them out (git-ignored; never published). Fastfile: magic `TAff0000`, 0x248-byte header, then blocks of `[compressedSize, decompressedSize, storedSize (4-aligned), ownFileOffset]` + zlib. zm_castle has 1,716 light records of 564 bytes, each ending in an id word (`0x30000 | index`). From a record's start: +104 type (2 spot, 4 omni), +112 origin (inches, Z up), +128 colour, +144 near, +148 radius, +196 unit vector toward the light, +308/+312 cos of the inner/outer cone, +384 lighting-state mask (bit 0 = on by default; 0 = switched by script, left out). Game to map: `(x, z, -y)`.
- **Bake** (`.tools/bake-map-light.mjs`, a map entry's `real: {file, gain, minRadius, albedo, bounces, adapt, fill, outdoor}`; flags `--gain --albedo --bounces --adapt --fill --outdoor --out dir/ --cache file`): the lights replace the guessed lamp positions (1,209 of them reach the play area). Each is a cone or a point with the falloff `(1-(d/r)^4)^2/d^2` and occlusion rays; a gather bounce (24 rays a cell) stands in for the lightmap's bounce light; `adapt: [ref, pw]` compresses levels above `ref` like the game's eye adaptation (`k = (L/ref)^(pw-1)`, so a lamp pool is a few times its room and not thirty); `fill: [level, r, g, b]` tops every indoor cell up to a floor in a cool grey (the real lights reach only about a third of the indoor volume directly; the game fills the rest with lightmap GI); `outdoor: cap` limits lamp light where the sky is visible (cap / sky), which is what stops snow by a lamp going white. Der Eisendrache: `gain 550, adapt [6, .45], fill [1.5, .84, .93, 1.1], outdoor 1.4`.
- **Rebake** (about 20 s with the caches, under a memory cap): `systemd-run --user --scope -q -p MemoryMax=6G -p MemorySwapMax=2G nice -n 15 node .tools/bake-map-light.mjs der-eisendrache --cache scratch/ff/sky.cache`. The bake is live as soon as it is written.

These are uniforms and data: no shader text changed, so players' shader caches stay warm.
- **Gated lights** (2026-11-12, `real.gated: {box, blue, keep, fill}`): inside `box`, lights bluer than `blue` (b > r x 1.3) go into the powered set B, less a dim copy `keep` ([level, r, g, b] of their luminance) left in the always set A; the box's other lights go fully into A; the adapt lift of A uses A's own level there and the room fill is `fill`, measured against A alone. At runtime a map can own the powered set: `light.power = base => value` (`map-volume.js`; `base` is 1 with the power on, else `powerOff`), eased at 1.5/s into `mvPower`. ## Real light shafts (2026-11-17, `mods/lighting/shafts.js`)

User: "something that has actual light beams instead of workarounds for it". The beam cones (`beamCone`, the baked `sunshafts.json`) are shapes placed by hand or by a bake: nothing can block them. `shafts.js` is the real thing: for each registered shadow-casting light, a full-screen pass walks every pixel's view ray up to the scene depth (28 jittered samples, spread by how much each stretch of air shows through) and asks the light's own shadow map whether that point of air is lit. Walls, props, zombies and players in the shadow map cut gaps into the light; a window or a roof hole lets a shaft into a dark room; no placement, no bake.

- **Where it runs**: inside `VolumetricPass` (mod.js), into the same half-size target as the beam cones, added onto the frame before AO and bloom. That add is now four bilinear taps (a 4x4 box), which smooths the sample jitter (and softens the cones a little). High and Ultra only (`shafts.scale`, set by the pass). Its own `ShaderMaterial` (one program, compiled behind the loading screen by a zero-colour draw against a 1x1 depth texture): no lit shader changed.
- **Lights**: a light shows shafts only while it casts shadows and has a PCF shadow map (`shadow.map.depthTexture` read as `sampler2DShadow`). Every registered sun is drawn, and the two nearest registered spots. Kino registers its day sun (lighting mod; spot lights are supported by the pass but none is registered: untested with the power on); Gorod Krovi registers its key light (`sun.js`, `SHAFTS`): its static shadow map, so the town cuts the shafts but zombies do not. Another map: `kino.shafts?.add(light, { ... })` once its sun casts shadows.
- **Kino's baked sun cones step aside**: while the sun's real shafts are drawn (`shafts.covers(sun)`, High/Ultra), the `kind:'sun'` beams fade to nothing; on Medium they stay as before. The viewmodel's sun key still reads the baked table (`b.live`).
- **Settings per light** (live in `kino.shafts.lights`): `strength` (the colour a fully lit endless ray adds, times the light's colour and intensity), `density` (extinction per unit), `g` (forward scattering), `far`, `dust` (drifting unevenness), `lift` (0 = plain physics; m' = m(1+L)/(1+Lm) on the lit share m of the ray, so a mostly shadowed ray, i.e. a thin indoor shaft, is raised toward a fully lit one, which stays as it is; 3 on Kino, 5 on Gorod), `sky` (share kept in front of the sky), `indoor` (a sun's shafts are this many times stronger while the camera stands in its shadow: 32 shadow-map reads around the camera in the vertex stage, an eye-adaptation stand-in), `white` (pulls the air's colour toward grey). `kino.shafts.enabled = false` for a before/after.
- **Tuning lessons**: one strength cannot serve open ground and a dark room (a room's shaft is a tenth as deep as open air), hence `lift` and `indoor`. Lifting by the ray's total light veiled every nearby shaded wall; keeping only part of the glow on fully lit rays inverted the contrast (edges glowed): the lift works on the lit *share* of the ray. Strengths that look right are small (0.012 for Gorod's intensity-2 key, 0.045 for Kino's 3.4 sun); 0.5 turned the whole view yellow.
- **Cost**: no measurable frame-time difference on the host's GTX 1660 Ti (24.4-31 ms with, 24.6-30 without, same page).
- **Checked** (routed, then live, real GPU, Ultra, 0 errors): Kino's lobby shows the sun as rays from the tall window down to the floor's sun patches, the alley's opening shows graded sunlit air, sunless rooms show nothing; Gorod (light copy) gets a faint warm haze toward the sun that buildings cut. Spot-light shafts were not checked (the test never turns the power on).

## Motion blur, aim depth of field, reflections (2026-11-19, `mods/lighting/post-fx.js`, user: "do all these")

Three passes in the lighting mod's composer, made by `createPostFx(api)` in `setupPost` and added in `build(q)`. Each skips its draw when it has nothing to do, so a still camera at the hip on a dry map pays nothing. Live handles in `kino.postFx` (`config`, `stats` of the last frame, `rules`, `addWater`, `addFloor`).

Order: RenderPass → volumetric → haze → **reflections** (High and Ultra) → GTAO (Ultra) → **motion blur** → viewmodel → **depth of field** → bloom → grade.

- **Buffers**: the world's depth stays in the buffer the RenderPass drew into (`fx.track(renderPass)` notes it as `sceneRT`). A pass can't draw into a buffer whose depth texture it samples, so each effect draws into its own target (`tmp`, or `half` for reflections) and copies or blends back. At Ultra, GTAO's swap puts the frame back on `sceneRT`, where the viewmodel pass would clear the world's depth: while aiming, the motion pass swaps too (`motion.needsSwap = sceneRT === rb && wantDof()`), so the gun is drawn on the other buffer.
- **Motion blur** (Settings → Motion blur: Off / Light / Full, default Light, `settings.motionBlur`): camera blur only. Each pixel's view position (from depth) is reprojected with last frame's view-projection, and 10 jittered taps run along that screen velocity (at most 4.5% of the screen). It sits before the viewmodel pass, so the gun and arms stay sharp. The strength follows the frame rate (a 60 fps shutter: `(1/60)/dt`, clamped) and halves at full aim. Not drawn when the estimated shift is under 1.2 px or the camera jumped over 150 units (teleports).
- **Depth of field** (Settings → Aim depth of field, `settings.dof`): runs only while `view.aim` ≥ .03 and the viewmodel is drawn. Gun pixels (the viewmodel's depth, `tView`) blur by how close they are to the eye: `dofGun` 7 px at 1080p at `gunNear` 3 units, sharp from `gunSharp` 13 (sights and the front of the gun stay sharp). World pixels blur by `dofWorldK * |1/z - 1/focus|`, at most `dofWorld` 2.4 px; `focus` is the nearer of the map ray-cast and `enemies.rayHit` along the view, eased. 16 taps; a sample only spreads onto a pixel as far as its own blur reaches, so the sharp background doesn't bleed into the gun.
- **Reflections** (Settings → Reflections, `settings.reflections`; half resolution on High, full on Ultra): screen-space, only where a rule says so, at most the 8 nearest rules a frame. A **water** rule (`addWater({y, rect:[x0,z0,x1,z1], strength, ripple, scale, f0, reach})`) covers pixels whose surface lies under the level `y` inside the rectangle, seen from above: the water mesh itself is transparent and not in the depth buffer, so the mirror plane is the level, tilted by animated ripples. A **floor** rule (`addFloor({min, max, strength, ripple, scale, f0, puddle, reach})`) covers upward-facing ground inside a box, optionally only in noise-shaped puddles. The view ray is mirrored, marched 26 growing steps through the depth buffer with a 5-step refinement, and laid over the pixel by Fresnel (`f0` straight on, rising to full at a low angle); a ray that ends on a sky pixel reflects the sky; hits within 3-22 units fade (a wall meeting the floor drew a dotted line). A map entry can carry `reflect: {water: [...], floors: [...]}`. **Gorod Krovi** registers its flood from the water meshes' own bounds (`seeThroughWater`, look.js: 11 stretches, the main one at y -180).
- All five programs (three effects, copy, blend) are drawn once at load by `fx.warm()` into targets of the formats used in play. Programs on sandbox at Ultra: 173 → 178.
- Tests (GTX 1660 Ti, Vulkan, 1280x720, Ultra): sandbox spin 16.7 px of blur with the gun sharp, 0 when still; aim blur on a real aim (`stats.dof` 1, focus on the far wall); Gorod's basement flood mirrors the far shelves and shows almost nothing straight down. Test runner: `scratch/fx/run.mjs` with `ROUTE=/path=file` copies and a `PLAY` script.
- Mistake to avoid: a `return` left in front of an inserted statement made the rule's distance test return the distance itself, so the pool the camera stood in (distance 0) was the one dropped.
