# Weapon levels, attachments and scopes

## Scopes (`mods/scopes`, all maps)

The exported viewmodels kept scope lenses opaque, so aiming a scoped gun showed a black disc.
- **Glass**: materials matching `lens(?!_interior)|reflex_lens` become transparent (0.1). The model's dot cards (`red_dot|reflex_red|aimpoint_red|scope_pka_crosshair`) have mixed backgrounds (some glowed as red blobs), so they're hidden. A CSS `#reflex-dot` is drawn at the screen centre while aiming a reflex/red dot sight (aim > .85).
- **Sight alignment** (reflex/red dot/holo, incl. mounted BO3 sights): `measure()` finds the dot/lens centroid in view space and the pivot is shifted so it sits on the screen centre. It re-measures every frame while aim > .6 and eases toward the result (a one-off measure at aim .97 caught the BO3 ADS pose and mounted parts still settling). `measure()` first calls `view.pivot.updateMatrixWorld(true)`: `view.update` moves the pivot but only refreshes the gun below it, so without that the measure saw last frame's offset and the loop settled halfway. `kino.scopes` exposes `align`, `measure`, `view` for tests.
- **Magnified optics** (`BUILT_IN`: Dragunov `pso`, L96A1 `duplex`, G11 `lps`, AUG `acog`; plus `def.scope` from attachments: `acog`, `ir`): once `view.aim` > .92, `host.hideViewmodel` is set and a canvas overlay draws a black housing with a round window and that optic's reticle (dark lines get a faint light halo so they read in dark rooms). Zoom per kind in `MAGNIFIED` (it also sets the built-in guns' `adsFov`). Sway moves the real aim (camera rotation, undone each frame). Holding Shift holds your breath for 3 s (sway ×0.08), then a 1.6 s gasp (×2.2). IR adds a CSS grayscale/contrast filter on the canvas and white emissive on zombie materials (restored after).
- **Sight alignment** (unmagnified): at full aim, the centroid of the visible sight's dot/lens vertices is measured in view space and the gun pivot is shifted so it sits on the screen centre, and at least 9 units from the eye. Gotchas: every surface shares one vertex buffer, so iterate the mesh's **index**. Hidden attachments are collapsed onto bones scaled 1e-6, so skip meshes whose dominant-weight bone chain has a ~0 scale.

## Weapon levels (`mods/weapon-levels`)

- `attachments.json`: XP (kill 10, headshot +5), curve (120·L^1.25), slots (optic, muzzle, mag, under), `mountTags` (rails shown with any optic), `builtInOptic` (no optic slot), and the attachment catalog in **unlock order**. Each entry has the tags it shows (plus `extraTags`), stats (`damageMul`, `rangeMul`, `clipMul` (maxAmmo grows by the same amount), `reloadMul`, `adsFov`/`adsFovMul`, `headMul`, `hipSpreadMul`, `suppressed`), `scope` and `underbarrel`.
- An attachment's `tags` are alternatives: only the first one the gun has is shown (the Galil carries both the Elbit and PKA reflex sights). `extraTags` and rail `mountTags` are shown when present.
- A weapon's attachments are the catalog entries whose tags appear in its default `hideTags` (BO1 viewmodels carry every attachment, hidden by tag). Level n unlocks the (n−1)th; max level = count + 1.
- `gunsmith.js` (shared by the mod and the main menu): profile fields `weaponXp[id]={level,xp}` and `attachments[id]=[ids]` (one per slot) in `kino.mods.progression`, so they're cloud-synced. `applyAttachments` restores each def (and its `upgrade`) from a pristine snapshot, then applies stats, removes shown tags from `hideTags`, sets `scope`, `attachments` and `hipSpread`, and builds `def.attachment` for underbarrels. The upstream key-5 toggle (`session.toggleAttachment`) then works without Pack-a-Punch. Templates: the M16's upgraded GL and the AUG's upgraded Masterkey (own animations only for those two guns; others keep their normal ones), with base stats GL 450 dmg 1+6 and Masterkey 150×8, 4+20.
- The mod: XP on `kill` when the last damage cause was `bullet`/`projectile`/`thunder`, or `explosion` while holding a launcher (not melee, not remote, not cheats). New attachments are auto-equipped if their slot is free, with a toast. It wraps `view.equip` to re-show bones of attachment tags that the current def doesn't hide (viewmodels are cached, so earlier hidden scales stick). Suppressed shots play at 0.28 volume (it wraps `audio.weapon`/`play`). Shows a `#weapon-level` HUD, and a pause-menu `#gunsmith-panel` for the gun in hand (refreshed when `#menu` unhides). `window.kino.weaponLevels.grant(id, xp)` is for testing.
- Main menu: `renderDetail` in home.js appends `renderGunsmith` for the focused weapon.
- Verified headlessly: an M16 with reflex+suppressor+extmag+GL has clip 45, damage 85, adsFov 46, and key 5 → "M16 · Grenade Launcher" 1/6. Three kills from 110 XP → level 2, Reflex unlocked and equipped. Sniper/ACOG/LPS overlays and the reflex/red dot alignment were checked in screenshots on the M16, Galil, AK-74u and MP5K. npm test passes (65).
- **Not done**: flamethrower underbarrel (no flame weapon exists), variable zoom, attachments on third-person/world models.

## Flamethrower underbarrel (`mods/weapon-levels/flamer.js`)

BO1 reference (Call of Duty wiki via search): an underbarrel on assault rifles except the G11, 100 fuel per tank at 4 per trigger pull (25 bursts), 600 RPM, ~12.1 m range, 50 damage per hit. Here: `flamerDef` builds the key-5 attachment def (clip 25 + 75 spare, fireTime .1, damage 1 so the hitscan does nothing). On each `session.shots` increment it `scorch`es every zombie within 475 units in a 22° cone, with line of sight and a wall-limited reach: 45 damage (cause `flame`, which counts toward weapon XP) plus ignition, which burns for 3 s at 55/s with flames on the body. Visuals use the game's own flipbooks: `fxt_fire_flame_base_01` (4×4 burn-out, additive), `fxt_fire_flame_lick` (16 variations), and `fxt_smk_flamethrower` (8×8 fireball→smoke, alpha) at the stream's end. They're emitted at ~95/s while firing from the nozzle's screen position (`tag_ft_flash`, then `tag_flame_unit`, then `tag_flash`, projected with a FOV-51 view camera and unprojected into the world). Sound: a synthesized looped roar into `audio.master` (the game data has no flamethrower loop), and the normal shot cue is muted. There's a pilot-light sprite on `tag_flamer_pilot_light` when the model has one. Tags: `tag_flame_unit` plus the bracket, bottle, button, ring, heat guard and pilot light.

## Gunsmith view (`mods/weapon-levels/gunsmith-view.js`)

A full-screen, BO7-style Gunsmith opened from Main menu → Loadout (weapon detail → OPEN GUNSMITH, with ◀ ▶ through every gun that has attachments) and from the pause menu (the gun in hand). It has its own WebGLRenderer. `loadModel(def.model)` (the viewmodel gun, which carries every attachment) is turned so the barrel runs along +X and centred; parts are shown or hidden by bone scale from `previewDef`. Left: slot rows with SVG leader lines (dashed; solid orange when selected) to screen-projected markers on the part each slot edits. The marker is the centroid of the fitted part's skinned vertices (index-based, cached in gun space), with a bone fallback for empty slots. Selecting a slot eases the camera onto that part and pulses its meshes (emissive, found by each mesh's dominant bone; materials are shared with the game, so they're restored on close). Right: that slot's options with pros/cons; hovering previews the part on the model and shows green/red stat deltas. Esc/BACK closes; clicks don't reach the game. `available()` uses the pristine `hideTags`, because applying attachments un-hides tags. Before that fix, equipped attachments vanished from the Gunsmith and the weapon's max level shrank.

## Variable zoom, third person, co-op, Pack-a-Punch layer (2026-09-30, later)

- **Variable Zoom** (`vzoom`, optic, `builtIn: true`): the only optic kind allowed on `builtInOptic` guns (`available()` requires `builtInOptic.includes(id) === !!a.builtIn`). It shows `tag_scope_vzoom` and hides the stock scope via `replaces` (`tag_dragunov_scope`, the L96's `tag_scope`, `tag_scope_g11`), which pushes those tags onto `hideTags`. In the scopes mod, kind `vzoom` draws a mil-dot duplex with a magnification readout. While scoped, the mouse wheel (captured on window before the game's wheel-to-switch) steps `ZOOMS` 4×/6×/8×/10× (FOV 24/16/12/9) by writing `adsFov` on the def and its upgrade. Verified: 16 → 9 after two wheel-ups, and the weapon didn't switch.
- **Third person**: `holdWeapon` already hides parts from the current def's `hideTags`, which attachments rewrite. The characters mod's hold key now includes `data.weapons[id].attachments`, so changes re-hold the gun. Verified: an M16 with ACOG + suppressor + M203 in third person.
- **Co-op**: the pose `w` is now `id:u?:attachments` (older `id:u` still parses). Teammates' held guns get `window.kino.weaponLevels.hideTagsFor(id, ids)` (a `previewDef` from the pristine def), so each player shows their own attachments.
- **Pack-a-Punch shot layer** (`mods/pap-sound`): the upgraded-weapon "pew" isn't in this export's audio (nothing matches pap/upgrad in the manifest, and the game dumps aren't in this checkout). So `audio.weapon('shot')` on upgraded, non-wonder guns adds a synthesized layer: saw + detuned square sweeping 1.9 kHz → 260 Hz over 0.13 s through a band-pass sweeping 2.6 k → 700, quieter on rapid fire. The real layer is now in place: `pap-layer.ogg` and `pap-layer2.ogg`, two 0.55 s variants cut from a BO1 recording the user downloaded (youtube dSUVsrvncyU, via yt-dlp into ~/Downloads/pap_shot.wav; loudnorm −16 LUFS, 0.13 s fade). One is picked at random at ±3% pitch, gain 0.6 (0.45 on rapid fire). The synthesized sweep is only a fallback if they fail to load.

## Fixes after user testing (2026-09-30, later)

- **Weapon XP reverting**: the progression mod saved its own stale copy of the profile 1.5 s after each kill, overwriting weapon XP (it looked like "not levelling, bar resets"). Its `save` now takes `weaponXp`/`attachments` from storage first, and the main menu's `save` takes the stored `weaponXp`. Tests must wait out that 1.5 s debounce (verified: 12 kills + 3.5 s → level 2, 170 XP).
- **No auto-equip**: unlocking only toasts "… unlocked · equip it in the Gunsmith" (the user found auto-equip snappy).
- **Reflex glass**: dot cards are hidden **per mesh** on every viewmodel copy. A per-material "already patched" skip left them visible on later copies (a dark dotted disc blocking the sight).
- **Bigger mags and reloads**: reload animations move `tag_clip` (the stock mag); fitted mags hang off their own bones. While one is fitted, the stock mag meshes (dominant bone `tag_clip`) are hidden, and the fitted mag's bone is driven each frame from `tag_clip`'s animated world matrix × their rest offset (from `skeleton.boneInverses`). Only mags that an attachment added are moved: the stock-hidden sets (base and upgrade) are snapshotted before `apply()`. Built-in bigger mags keep their own animation (the Pack-a-Punched Commando's dual mag, the PM63 upgrade's extended mag); moving the Predator's had shifted it up. When a Pack-a-Punched gun shows its own bigger mag *and* a mag attachment is fitted (the user's Commando with extmag → Predator), the fitted mag follows the built-in mag's bone instead of `tag_clip`: the PaP reload animates that bone, and following `tag_clip` left the mag sticking out of the top. The built-in mag's meshes are hidden (its bone keeps scale 1, so its matrix stays usable). Verified with side renders mid-reload (M16, AK-74u, Galil dualmag, Commando extmag plain and Pack-a-Punched).
- **Gunsmith markers**: empty slots use a measured home (a representative attachment fitted for a moment at load). Only tags the gun hides by default count as slot parts (the M16's stock handguard is skinned to `tag_heat_guard`).
- **Loadout**: GUNSMITH buttons on the Primary/Secondary cards; the detail panel's Gunsmith is only for the weapon in the selected slot; hovering the grid previews another gun and leaving returns to the equipped one; the Gunsmith's arrows cycle only primary/secondary (hidden if there's one).
- **Iron sights with optics**: `attachments.json` `ironSights` maps a gun to its iron-sight bones (front and rear), which `foldIronSights` hides when any optic is fitted, so the front post doesn't show through the sight window. That covers the Galil, MP5K, M14, M16, Commando, FAMAS, Spectre, FAL, MPL, AK-74u, HK21 and RPK. On the M16/Commando the bone is the carry handle plus the front post, and on the FAMAS it's the handle; the optics don't hang off those bones (verified hip + ADS, 2026-09-30).
- **Box and wall guns** (2026-10-01): attachments are saved per gun and applied to `data.weapons` at load, so a gun from the box or a wall comes with them (verified: a Galil with reflex + dualmag rolled from the real box arrived with both, rear sight folded). The main-menu Gunsmith's arrows cycle every gun with attachments (loadout first), and the header labels each PRIMARY / SECONDARY / BOX / WALL GUN (`label` option of `openGunsmith`), so guns can be set up before they're found. The pause-menu Gunsmith still edits the gun in hand.

## Camos (`mods/weapon-levels/camo.js`, `camos.json`; 2026-10-01)

- Per-gun challenges counted on eligible gun kills (same rule as weapon XP, but for any gun): `kills`, `headshots`, `low` (prone or crouched), `moving` (sliding or diving), `multi` (another kill with the same gun within 1.5 s). Saved in `profile.camo[id] = {p:{counts}, on:camoId}`. Each camo has a challenge and count; `mastery` (Gold) unlocks when every other camo is done. Unlocks toast "… camo unlocked · equip it in the Gunsmith" (never auto-equipped).
- Placeholders are drawn on canvases (woodland, tiger, digital, urban, red tiger, gold). A real camo is `"texture": "camos/<file>.png"` (tileable, next to camos.json), replacing `procedural`; `scale` is UV repeats and `mix` is coverage; `metal` raises metalness and lowers roughness (Gold).
- Painting: `applyCamo(root, camo)` swaps body materials for cached clones (keyed on original uuid + camo id; the original is kept in `userData.camoOf`, so null restores). The shader adds the camo after `map_fragment`: `mix(base, camo × (.3 + luminance × 1.9), mix)` on `vMapUv × scale`, so the gun's shading and wear show through. Body = has a map and the name doesn't match the SKIP list (sights, lenses, suppressors, underbarrels, straps, mag tape, mounts, rails, iron sights, hands…).
- Where it shows: the viewmodel (`view.equip` wrapper), the box display (wrapped `mysteryBox.display`), the third-person held gun (the characters mod's hold key includes `def.camo`), co-op (pose `w` gains a 4th field, the camo id) and the Gunsmith preview. `data.weapons[id].camo` is set in `apply()`. `window.kino.camo.apply(root, id)`.
- Gunsmith: a CAMO row under the attachments (swatch, `n / 6 UNLOCKED`); its options list each camo with its challenge and a progress bar, locked until earned, and hovering previews it on the 3D gun. The stats panel is hidden on that row.
- Profile saves keep camo: weapon-levels `save`, progression `write` (takes stored `camo`), and the menu's `save` (stored progress, menu's `on`).
- Counts raised at the user's request (2026-10-01): Woodland 500 kills, Tiger 200 headshots, Digital 150 low, Urban 75 moving, Red Tiger 40 multi-kills; Gold still needs all five.
- **Diamond** (`classMastery`, 2026-10-01): unlocks for every gun in a class once every gun in that class has Gold (all challenge camos done). Classes are shared in camo.js (`weaponClass`, `CLASSES`, `classOf`, `classGuns`; home.js imports them): AR 7, SMG 7, shotgun 4, LMG 2, sniper 2, pistol 4, launcher 4 (includes the crossbow and ballistic knife) in base game data. Mod-added guns count too (e.g. MP40 Drum, Golden Python, Moon weapons). No Diamond for Specials. `camoUnlocked`/`equippedCamo`/`countCamoKill` take `weapons`. The Gunsmith shows "Gold on every assault rifle · n / m", and the toast reads "Diamond camo unlocked for every assault rifle". Placeholder texture: faceted ice-blue and white triangles with sparkles, metallic.

## Camo wrap for the Advanced Warfare guns (2026-11-01)

AW atlases are cut into small islands (some 2:1), so UV-tiled camo broke at every seam, and their albedo runs from near-black to white, so the old `.3+L*1.9` shading made white guns twice as bright as black ones. `camos.json` `wrap` lists them; anything its `guns` regex doesn't match keeps the old path unchanged.

- **Projection**: `camoPlanar()` is triplanar in the mesh's bind pose (`vCamoP=position`, `vCamoN=normal`, not skinned, so a slide or mag carries its pattern). Frequency = `camo.scale * wrap.scale` per model unit: about 20 units a tile at the default 2.5, the same on every gun and on the world models. The Pack-a-Punch and prestige glows use it too.
- **Shade**: `albedo / its own blur` (mip bias 4) clamped to .6-1.3, times .85: panel lines and wear only, no overall brightness.
- **Skipped materials** (`wrap.skip`): ammo, speedloader, powder horn, optics, sights, screens, decals, text, emblem, the Sentry's barrels.
- **Kept parts** inside a material: a mask from the albedo at mip bias 1. `warm` ramps `(top-b)/top` with `top=max(r,g)` (wood, leather, brass, printed red and orange), `pale` ramps the mean (white labels). Default `warm` .6-.75 leaves tan polymer paintable (MDL). The wood guns use .2-.35 (M1 wood reads .3-.7 in linear). `pale` must stay off the white guns (Tac-19, CEL-3). Loot skins have `warm: null`: their red accents are paint. Kept parts also lose the metal camos' metalness and roughness (`camoMR`) and the glows.
- All of it is uniforms in the one camo program (`camoTri` 0 = old path), so nothing new has to be warmed up, and the data is only material names (no derived texture ships).
- Judge it at game light: scene lights boosted for a side-view render make the camo look washed out.

## Weapon charms (`mods/charms`, 2026-11-06, user: "keep the weapon screen in mind, im gonna go with simple charms to start off")

A charm hangs from a small ring on the gun's left side (the side the player sees) and swings. The system was built first, with no charms in it: the user uploads the first model.

- **Catalogue** `mods/charms/charms.json`. Each entry has `id`, `name`, `desc` and `model` (a GLB relative to the folder, normally `models/<id>.glb`). Optional fields:

- **State**: `kino.mods.charms` = `{on:{gunId:charmId}}`, cloud-synced (`cloud.js` TRACKED). The install manifest ships `charms.json`. A charm model needs its own `OWN` rule once it is known to be redistributable.
- **Shared code** `charms.js`:
  - `charmModel` normalises a model (scale to `size`, hang point at the origin) and caches it.
  - `gunAnchor` picks the hang spot. It samples the visible gun vertices through each surface's index (BO1 surfaces share one buffer; the SPAS parks spare shells 127 in behind, so it also filters |x| < 60 and uses percentile bounds). It skips dead attachment bones. Since 2026-11-06 (user: "it just kind of floats"), the ring always sits on a real vertex of the gun's outer surface on the visible side. For each x along the gun, it takes the outermost vertex every .15 in of height. Only points on the visible half count (outside the middle of the gun's width plus 15%: a point under the middle hangs the charm out of sight). Something wider below in the charm's column (`drop` and `width` from `charmReach`: the chain plus the body's height) is a cost (`lean`, 3 per inch), and a spot is dropped above .8 in: the charm would lie on it. The ring is never pushed out into the air. Parts that leave the gun in a reload (`LOOSE`: mags, shells, speedloaders, belts) and small parts that snap on every shot (`FIDGET`: hammer, trigger, safety, selector...) are never hung from. Points are scored on: hands in the way, off screen, lean, how far under the bore (less visible), distance from about 10% along, and +1 for a moving part. **Moving parts** (user: "if its on a moving part of the gun, like it is on the m1 irons, the charm moves with it"): each point records its bone (the dominant skin bone, or an unskinned part's nearest bone ancestor). When that is not the root, the anchor stores the ring and the gun's side/forward axes in that bone's own space (`local`, `n`, `f`; a bone's axes aren't aligned with the gun), and `CharmRig.step({bone})` follows the bone each frame (an M1911 hangs it from `j_bolt`, the slide). The M1 Irons hung it from the hammer, tucked away, until `FIDGET`; it now hangs from the frame. **Units**: the BO3/AW imports (`def.bo3`, Greyhound exports) are in centimetres and sit about 2.5x farther from the camera, so a charm came out at 40% size on them. `charmUnit(def)` (2.54 for `bo3`, `def.charmUnit` overrides) scales the rig (`step({unit})`) and the search runs in inches (`gunAnchor({unit})`: points divided by it, the result multiplied back). `anchors` overrides stay in the model's own units. The anchor is cached per gun model, hands, drop and shown attachments. The Gunsmith redoes the search with the picked charm's reach.
  - `CharmRig`: Verlet physics at 1/120 s with points for the ring, the chain end and the body's centre. Constraints: an angle limit of about 105° from gravity, and a side plane so the body never goes through the gun. The camera's turn is fed in (`moved`, follow .25, shift capped per frame), so turning swings it. Measured: it rests about 12° off vertical (the gun's cant), swings to about 31° at 230°/s and settles in about .3 s. **Stiffer since 2026-11-06** (user: "a tad bit less exaggerated... doesnt absurdly fling around when ADSing"): the gun's own motion in view space (ADS, recoil, sway) is carried rigidly at `carry` .8 (points and their previous positions both move, so no speed is added) and only the rest swings it; a spring (`stiff` .012 per 1/120 s step) pulls toward hanging straight; drag .035 → .07. `scratch/charms/ads.mjs` measured the largest swing from rest over two ADS cycles and a 230°/s turn: ADS 74-100° → 6-18°, turn about 14° → 6.5-8°.
  - Smoother since 2026-11-06 (user: it jittered when running with the charm pressed on the gun): the camera's push (`moved`) is spread over the frame's physics steps instead of one lump; on contact with the gun's side the point moves out with its previous position too (no bounce) and loses its speed into the gun; the drawn points are interpolated between the last two steps and then smoothed in the gun's (or the moving part's) frame, ~35 ms (`smooth` in charms.json), so the gun's own motion is not delayed. `scratch/charms/jit.mjs` (strafe/circle, jerk of the body in the ring's frame): jerk 20-130 → 9-13 on the M1911 and AK-74u; ADS swing unchanged (8-21°).
- **First person** `mod.js`:

The rig lives in `viewScene` and is placed 450 ms after the raise (the hands' grip is measured too). Only the held session gun gets a charm (not equipment, not the left gun of a dual pair).

Selecting the slot turns the camera to the charm side (`charmYaw`) and zooms in, and a kick swings it.

## Gun screens (`mods/charms/screens.js`, 2026-11-07, user: "build the gun screens. they'll be include in a new tab in the charms, and will connect to guns in a comfortable manner")

A small display fixed flat on the gun's left side (the side the player sees), one per gun, alongside its charm. The user picked four kinds to start ("basically all of these and more, but just these 4 for now") and the flush side mount. From the user's reference pictures: "set up the square looking one where the screen takes it all up, and make one with interactable buttons ... for future implementation". The user also allowed Blender for this.

- **Housings** are original Blender models, `.tools/build_gun_screens.py` → `mods/charms/screens/{slab,field}.glb` (installed, OWN rule). `blender -b -P .tools/build_gun_screens.py -- --render scratch/screens` also renders previews. Inches, face toward +Z, back at z=0, exported Z-up as built.
  - `slab` (1.2 × 1.2 in): the screen fills the face inside a thin raised rim. It has a trim band, grip ridges on the right edge and a status LED on top.
  - `field` (1.1 × 1.45 in): a screen in a raised trim frame on top, three buttons below. `btn_0` is round; `btn_1` and `btn_2` are the up and down halves of a rocker. Each button is its own node with its origin on the face, so a press is a move along -Z.
  - Every housing has `screen` (UVs 0..1, gets the display shader) and a mount (2026-11-07, user: "it doesnt really look like its attached to the gun ... something like a clip added to the back"; it replaced `foot`, a plain block that read as floating): a `boss` on the back, `arm` (z=0 to -1, scaled in z to reach the gun) and `clip`, an empty holding a clamp plate with a rubber pad, claws over its top and bottom edges, a ball socket and two screws (its own frame: back on the gun at z=0, +Z out, the arm's ball at z=.13). Materials: `shell`, `trim` (recoloured per entry: `trim`), `rubber`, `glyph`, `led`.
- **Catalogue** (`charms.json`):
  - `housings{id:{model,w,h,d,px,foot,buttons}}`, where `px` is the display's pixel size (slab 64×64, field 84×64).
  - `screens[]`: `id`, `name`, `desc`, `housing`, `kind`, `tint` (display colour), `trim`, `art`, `marquee`, `glow`, plus `unlock`/`pass`/`soon` as for charms (`charmOwned`).
  - `screenMounts{gunId:{at,size,tilt,yaw,foot}}` overrides the automatic spot.
  - Six screens, all Season 1 Melon Pass rewards since 2026-11-07 (`pass:"s2"`, `unlock: pass.s2.screen.<id>`, pass.md): Tally (page 1), Field Readout (2, ★), Melon Buddy (4), Field Tally (7, ★), Melon Loop (8), Field Buddy (9, ★). Locked ones show PASS in the SCREENS tab; `kino.dev.soon=1` lifts the lock for testing.
- **State**: `kino.mods.charms.screen{gunId:screenId}`, in the same cloud-synced key as the charms (`loadCharmState` defaults `screen`).
- **Kinds** (`KINDS` in screens.js; each has `draw(lcd, state, t, memory, entry)` and an optional `ev(memory, kind)`):
  - `art`: the built-in `melon` (a rocking pixel melon slice, twinkles and a scrolling marquee). Or a sprite sheet `{image, cols, rows, frames, fps}`, scaled to the screen once at load.
  - `kills`: the gun's lifetime kills and headshots, from the camo counters (`kino.weaponLevels.profile.camo[id].p`), so attribution matches the camo challenges. The box flashes on a kill or headshot.
  - `buddy`: a striped melon face with moods idle (blinks), happy (kill, +1), wow (headshot), hurt (shakes), reload (sweat drop), down (X eyes, HELP!), and sleepy after 25 s without an event. Higher-priority moods aren't interrupted.
  - `ammo`: round, the mag (big), the reserve, and a notch bar (one notch per round up to 40). Warnings: LOW (a quarter mag or less), EMPTY and RELOAD (with a sweep).
  - Events come from the game: a rise in the gun's kill or headshot count, a reload starting, `beforeDamage` (hurt), `session.phase === 'reviving'` (down), and `button`.
- **Drawing**: `Lcd` is a tiny RGBA pixel buffer (row 0 = the top: glTF UVs put v=0 at the image's top, so the rows are NOT flipped for GL; flipping them showed every screen upside down until 2026-11-07) with a 3×5 font (`FONT`, octal rows), rects, discs, lines and character sprites. It's redrawn at most 15 times a second and uploaded only when its hash changes (16-21 KB, `DataTexture`, nearest, no mipmaps). That's far under the 100 KB per-upload line in the performance rules; this replaced the earlier plan of shader-uniform text, because new kinds are plain JS this way.
  - The shader (`ShaderMaterial` `gun_screen`, one program for all screens) adds the LCD pixel grid (faded out by `fwidth` when the pixels are small on screen), the glow, a vignette and a glass sheen. It includes tone mapping and colour space.
- **Mount** (`screenMount`): runs in the root bone's space from `visiblePoints`. Pass the root's inverse world matrix as its `space` argument (2026-11-07: it used to read charms.js's private matrix, so screens were mounted in a stale space and sat off the gun).
  - **Face orientation**: the normal is tilted 20° up and yawed 42° back toward the eye. At 16°/12° the face was about 70° off the line of sight and read edge-on.
  - **Candidate grid**: the points are binned in 0.15 in cells over (x, z), keeping the outermost `p·n` per cell, plus loose-part (`LOOSE`/`FIDGET`), moving-bone and hand counts.
    - **Gap fill**: a flat panel has vertices only at its edges, so gaps of up to 1.5 in between backed cells are filled along x, then z, with the surface interpolated. Without this, a plain receiver side failed the coverage test and only 18 spots on the AK survived.
    - Candidates step 0.25 in (x) and 0.2 in (z), at sizes 1.25, 1.08, .93 and .78 (a quarter bigger since 2026-11-07, user: "just abit bigger ... the whole things"; was 1-.62); all sizes are scored.
  - **Rejected**: under 35% of the face backed (45% before the clip); under 70% of the foot backed; any loose part under the foot; a foot deeper than 0.9 in; facing the eye at under 0.3; hands hiding over 30% of it.
  - **The eye** is the view camera in game. The Gunsmith has no game camera, so it uses a typical hip eye at (xmin-9, 3.5, top+1.2). The eye's distance behind the gun ranges from 10 in (AK) to 20 in (Ray Gun).
  - **Surface sampling** (2026-11-07 pass, user: "some of the screens have awkward placements ... go gun by gun ... more in the middle, center of focus ... its alright for smaller guns like pistols and sidearms to be closer to the stock"): `visiblePoints(..., fill=.6)` (charms.js) also drops points inside long triangles (every .6 in, up to 40 per edge, 20,000 per gun, triangles over 30 in and points outside |x|<60, |y|,|z|<30 skipped: parked parts far off the gun blew up its length). The China Lake's left side has almost no vertices, so without this its screen sat inside the gun. Bins keep the lowest point too (`ylo`); the gap fill bridges up to 30 in, but only across flat panels when the gap is over 10 in. A spot needs at least 10% of its face over real (unfilled) cells.
  - **Cost**: the unbacked area ×3, the foot depth, moving parts, a hand touching or crowding the housing, and sticking above the bore (or sitting low under it). The main terms:
    - `50·max(0, .1 - width·facing/distance)`: how big it reads from the eye.
    - `10·max(0, |along - aT| - .07)`: aT = .3 from the butt on guns of 9 in or less (pistols sit near the grip), rising to .5 (mid-gun) at 16 in.
    - `1.2·|ndc - (.12, -.15)|`: the centre of focus, a little right of and below the crosshair.
    - `8·hidden` (hand points between the eye and a 4×4 grid over the face) and `6·gunHid` (5 face points marched toward the eye through the bins: a cell whose surface spans the ray means the gun itself covers it; over 30% rejects). The latter stops screens tucked behind a pistol's frame or a rifle's receiver.
    - The clip: unbacked plate ×3, spread of the gun under it, hook length past 1 in (no hook +.4), and an arm over .7 (×1.5): a long arm or a tall riser looks spindly.
  - **Per-gun nudges** in `charms.json` `screenMounts`: `along` (0 butt .. 1 muzzle) and `height` (0 bottom .. 1 top) bias the search; `at`/`size`/`tilt`/`yaw`/`foot` pin it. Dragunov and L96A1 use `along .25` (their automatic spots were small and edge-on, or far forward under the scope).
  - **Baked spots** (2026-11-07, user: "make it consistent with the gunsmith please, no need to have it search during play"): the Gunsmith (no arms, its own camera) and the game (arms and view camera, searched at the first raise: 100-350 ms, a hitch) used to search separately and could pick different spots. Now `.tools/bake-screen-spots.mjs` runs the search once per gun model (base and Pack-a-Punched) and housing in a game page, with the player's arms and the view camera and always clear of the charm's hang zone, and writes `mods/charms/screen-spots.json` (`model|housing` -> `{c, s, t, depth, hook, tilt, yaw, along, cost}` in the root bone's space, inches). `loadCharms()` reads it as `catalogue.screenSpots`; `screenMount(...,{spots})` turns a spot into the mount (`mountFromSpot`, `spotKey`) in both places. A model missing from the file falls back to the plain search (no arms, no camera, the same in both), which the game runs in its `warmWeapon` handler, at load. An `at` override still pins the spot. **Rebake** (`~/.local/bin/lowpri node .tools/bake-screen-spots.mjs [--force] [ids...]`; already-baked models are skipped) after changing a gun model, a housing, the search, or a `screenMounts` `along`/`height` hint. About 10 min for the 200-odd models, Sandbox map with `?noWarm`. The bake decodes every image at 4x4: the page loads every weapon model at start and holds their decoded bitmaps until the main renderer uploads them (3-4 GB, see performance.md, Decoded weapon bitmaps).
  - **Off screen and charm clearance**: corners off screen add 2 each (in game). When the gun has a charm, its plain hang spot (`charmZone`, the same in the Gunsmith and the game) with a 3 in drop and 0.45 in half width adds 5.
  - **Hook** (2026-11-07, user: "the claw that connect it to the gun doesnt seem to really work ... add abit more to the back part that makes it actually look like its connected"): the clip also has `clip_riser` (a stout 0.5 × 0.12 in bar in the dark shell; a thin gold one read as a coat hanger), `clip_lip` and `clip_tip`. `landing()` returns `hook {len, depth}` when the gun's top edge is within reach above the plate: the riser is stretched up past the top (`setMount`: scale.y = len + .12) and the lip reaches over it, so the clamp visibly grips the receiver. No top edge in reach = no hook, just the plate.
  - **Placement**: the back plane sits 0.03 in outside the outermost point. The arm runs straight back (-n) from the housing to the clip's ball; the clip lies flat on the gun's side (normal +y in root space) on the outermost gun point within its footprint (searched 2.5x and 5x wider when the panel has no vertices there), found in 3 passes since where the arm lands depends on the depth. An arm under 0.22 in (× size) pushes the housing out so the arm shows. `screenMount` returns `arm` (housing units) and `clip {p, q}` (housing frame); `placeScreen` applies them through `GunScreen.setMount` when the mount changes, and `along` (0 butt .. 1 muzzle) for tests. Spots come from the bake (see Baked spots); a search result is cached in memory per model, housing, override and hidden bones.
  - **Results** (2026-11-07 sweep, 31 guns, `scratch/screens/r3/run.mjs gun:screen ...`, hip | close | side frames): rifles, SMGs and shotguns get a full-size screen on the receiver side beside the left hand, with the hook over the top; pistols sit by the grip and read smaller (M1911, Python .78). The Dragunov and L96A1 needed `along` overrides.
- **Game** (`charms/mod.js`): mounts when the held gun changes (450 ms after the raise, like the charm), from the baked spot (`mountFor`, the same call as the Gunsmith's: no arms or camera). The screen is a child of the view scene with `matrix = root.matrixWorld × mount` each frame (`placeScreen`), and is hidden with the gun.   - Console: `kino.charms.setScreen(id|null)`, `kino.charms.press(i)` (presses a housing button, sends `button` to the content), `.display` (the GunScreen), `.screenMount`.
- **Gunsmith**: the CHARM row counts charms and screens together and shows both names. The panel has CHARMS | SCREENS tabs (`data-chtab`); options are `data-screen`, tagged with the kind. Hovering previews, locked ones too.
  - The preview shows demo data: this gun's real counts, a full mag, and the buddy's moods cycling every 2.6 s.

## Weapon stickers (`mods/stickers`, 2026-11-06, user: "you can freely place 3 stickers on the weapon, having it wreap around it accordingly, you can also resize the stickers abit")

- **Catalogue** `mods/stickers/stickers.json` (`id`, `name`, `desc`, `image` relative to the mod, optional `soon`/`pass`/`unlock` with the charm rules: `stickerLock`). Images are 256 px RGBA PNGs packed into one atlas CanvasTexture (`stickerAtlas`, 256 px cells, 12 px padding). The test sticker `smiley` comes from the user's image; `images/` is git-ignored and not in Rind installs until the art's origin is known.
- **Saved** per gun base id in `kino.mods.stickers` (cloud-tracked): `{on:{<gunId>:[{id,k,p,n,t,s,m,r}]}}`. Up to `MAX_STICKERS` = 3. `k` = the mesh key it was placed on (`name#n`, mounted parts skipped); `p`/`n`/`t` = bind-space point, normal and tangent; `s` = half size in geometry units; `m` = the size slider (0.5-2); `r` = rotation in degrees.
- **Drawing**: no decal meshes. `stickerMaterial` chains an `onBeforeCompile` on the gun's own material (program key `|stk`): a box projection in bind space (`position`/`normal` attributes, so it follows skinned parts and wraps over curves), alpha from the atlas, a facing fade (`smoothstep(.08,.3,dot(N,z))`) so it doesn't come through the far side, metalness × (1-a), roughness toward .5 and the emissive cut under the sticker. Each mesh gets the stickers of the meshes sharing its position buffer (imported guns: a sticker stays on its part). Lenses, reticles and dot cards (`SKIP`) and materials without a map are left alone. Changing the size, angle or position only updates uniforms.
- **Camos**: a sticker material keeps `userData.camoOf` pointing at the original, so `applyCamo` rebuilds the camo and then calls `restick(root)` (the root carries `userData.kinoStickers`).
- **Game**: weapon-levels `mod.js` applies them after the camo on equip (both guns of a dual wield), in `warmWeapon` (its program compiles while loading) and on the `kino-stickers` event the Gunsmith sends.
- **Gunsmith**: COSMETIC → STICKERS · n / 3. **Drag and drop** (user, 2026-11-06: "placement should be drag and drop, moveable that way"): drag a tile from ADD onto the gun (a ghost follows the pointer; over the gun the sticker itself previews, one ray a frame), release to place; drag a placed sticker on the gun to move it (a `grab` cursor shows over one; `stkUnder` = same vertex buffer, within s×m of its centre, facing within 78°); a drag anywhere else turns the gun as before. A tap on a tile then a tap on the gun also places it (touch). Right-click or Esc cancels. Rows: REMOVE; the selected row has SIZE (50-200%) and ROTATE (±180°) sliders, saved on release. With all three placed, a new one replaces the selected one. Placement size = 18% of the gun's radius at 100%.
- **Which side** (fixed 2026-11-06): first person sees the gun's *left* side (the camera sits left of a right-handed gun); the Gunsmith's default view shows the other one. Opening STICKERS turns to the in-game side (`STK_GAME` yaw, label "SIDE SEEN IN GAME"), FLIP turns to `STK_OTHER` ("OTHER SIDE · NOT SEEN IN GAME"); the label follows the target yaw, not the camera passing by (it used to read the wrong side mid-turn). A drag stops the turn.
- **Hidden stickers**: each row checks its sticker's world normal (`stkWorld`: bind-space centre and normal through `applyBoneTransform` of the nearest vertex) against the in-game camera direction; under -0.25 it shows NOT SEEN IN GAME and a MOVE TO GAME SIDE button (`stkToGame`: a ray from that side through the sticker's centre, `stickerFromHit` with a camera there, size and turn kept). Not the cause of the "under the camo" report (below).
- **Stickers missing on camoed guns (fixed 2026-11-06)**: `Material.clone()` deep-copies `userData` through `JSON.stringify`, and a camo material's `userData.camoOf` is the original material, so three serialised its textures to data URLs; on the main menu those are ImageBitmaps that texture-memory.js has already closed, so the clone threw ("image source is detached") and the sticker material was never built: the gun kept its plain camo in the main-menu Gunsmith on every camoed gun. Clone materials with `cloneMaterial()` (stickers.js: clones with `userData` set aside; camo.js uses it too), never `m.clone()` on a material whose userData holds objects. Tests: `document.getElementById('gsv').debug` gives the Gunsmith's live `gun`, `renderer` and `camera`.
- **Gold shine** (2026-11-07, user: "give the gold on the crest one a sort of glimmer? like it was drawn with a gold pen"): a catalogue entry with `shine: "gold"` (Toll Crest; Bee Seal since 2026-11-07, user: "bee seal can get the glimmer too") sets `stkF[i]` = 1 for its slot (`atlas.shine`). `stkOne` finds the gold ink by colour: linear saturation smoothstep .38-.62 and r>=g>b (the crest's paper sits at .2-.3, its gold lines .45-.9). On the ink: metalness .55, roughness .28, and an emissive glimmer added before `lights_fragment_begin`: a narrow sheen band that sweeps across the sticker (`stkTime`*.16, so every ~6 s) and whose phase also follows the view-space normal (turning the gun slides it), plus soft pinpoint flecks on a 64-cell grid (14% of cells, each twinkling on its own phase). `stkTime` is one shared uniform set in the sticker materials' `onBeforeRender`. Program key is now `|stk2` (one program for all stickers, shine or not). The Gunsmith tiles of a shine sticker get a CSS sweep (`.stk-gold`, masked by the image). Images are drawn into the atlas in proportion (letterboxed), so non-square stickers like Past Due keep their shape. Flat-panel render test: `scratch/stickers/flat.mjs` (`STK=<id>`), menu Gunsmith: `scratch/stickers/gold.mjs`.
- **Season 1 stickers** (the user's, 2026-11-07): Past Due, High Voltage, Bee Seal, Sprouted Skull, Rain Meter, Toll Crest (gold shine), `pass:"s2"`, unlock `pass.s2.sticker.<id>`, shop catalog `cat:"sticker"`, `data.sticker`.
